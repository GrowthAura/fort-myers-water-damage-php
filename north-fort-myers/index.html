Key facts about water damage in North Fort Myers

  • Many residents live in manufactured-home and 55+ communities, where water soaks particle board decking and collects in the belly wrap.
  • Homes along the Caloosahatchee and its creeks face surge and high water during hurricanes.
  • Water heaters in small closets beside bathrooms and kitchens are a common source of soft floors.
  • Seasonal residents mean leaks can run unnoticed for weeks.

Water Damage in North Fort Myers Right Now? Do This First

  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main. In a manufactured home, check under the home near where the supply line comes in. On a well, switch off the pump at its breaker.
  • Cut power at the breaker to wet rooms. In a manufactured home, make sure no wiring under the home is sitting in water before anyone crawls under.
  • Turn off the water heater if it's leaking, at its breaker or gas control.
  • Stay off soft spots in the floor.
  • Photograph everything before cleanup, including under the home if it's safe.
  • In a land-lease community, tell the office if the leak is at the lot's water or sewer connection.
  • Get the belly and floor checked the same day; particle board decking softens fast.

What Makes Water Damage Different in North Fort Myers?

  • Manufactured homes. Particle board or OSB decking, insulation in the floor system and a belly wrap underneath. Water soaks into the floor and gets trapped in the belly. See mobile home water damage.
  • The Caloosahatchee and its creeks. North Fort Myers runs along the river's north bank. During hurricanes, surge and high water push up the river and into creeks such as Hancock Creek and Daughtrey's Creek, reaching low-lying streets.
  • Wells and septic. Larger-lot neighborhoods often have a private well, pressure tank, softener and septic system, each its own source of leaks and backups.
  • Older slab homes. Some neighborhoods have original plumbing, cast iron drains and aging water heaters. See cast iron pipe water damage.
  • Seasonal residents. Many homes sit empty part of the year. See water damage in vacant homes.

Who Supplies Your Water in North Fort Myers?

North Fort Myers doesn't have one water provider, and knowing yours tells you who to call about a leak at the meter or a backup in the sewer line.

  • Lee County Utilities serves many unincorporated neighborhoods.
  • FGUA (Florida Governmental Utility Authority) runs the former North Fort Myers Utility system, which provides sewer and, in some areas, water.
  • Private wells and septic are common on larger lots, such as in Suncoast Estates.

On a well, a pump that runs when no water is in use, or a pressure tank that keeps cycling, can point to a hidden leak. A septic tank that backs up after heavy rain needs pumping or drain field work before indoor cleanup is finished.

Manufactured-Home Communities in North Fort Myers: Who Fixes What?

In a land-lease community, where you own the home and rent the lot, Florida Statute 723.022 requires the park owner to keep the utility connections and systems it's responsible for in proper operating condition. Leaks in the home's own plumbing, water heater and floor are usually the homeowner's. Exactly where the line falls is set out in your prospectus and park rules, so check them, tell the community office about a leak at the connection and photograph both sides. Owners on their own land, as in Suncoast Estates, handle everything on the lot.

Manufactured Homes in North Fort Myers: Where Does the Water Go?

  • The floor decking, which swells and goes soft, often near the water heater, toilet, kitchen sink or washer.
  • The insulation in the floor system, which holds water against the decking and framing.
  • The belly wrap, the bottom board under the home, which can fill with water and sag.

Drying usually means opening the belly, removing wet insulation and replacing damaged decking, not just running fans inside. See mobile home water damage.

Flooding Along the Caloosahatchee in North Fort Myers

When rising water reaches a home, it's floodwater, Category 3, even if it looks clear. Wet drywall, insulation, carpet and particle board usually come out before drying. In manufactured homes, floodwater fills the belly and soaks the floor system from below. Flood damage is handled by a flood policy, not standard homeowners insurance, and new flood policies usually have a 30-day waiting period. On a well, have the water tested before drinking it after a flood.

Seasonal and 55+ Communities in North Fort Myers

Before leaving for the summer, shut off the main water (or the well pump) and the water heater, set the AC to control humidity and arrange regular checks. After a storm, have someone look at the home as soon as it's safe. If your community office requires notice of contractor work, have your lot number ready when you call.

Permits and the 50% Rule in North Fort Myers

North Fort Myers is unincorporated, so permits for repairs, and the 50% rule for homes in a flood zone, go through Lee County's building department, not a city. Under that rule, if repairs reach half the building's market value, the home has to be brought up to current flood standards. Keep photos and invoices for every repair. Insurance for North Fort Myers Water Damage

This is general information, not advice about your policy.

  • A water heater or supply line that fails suddenly is often covered for the damage it caused; floor rot from a slow leak usually isn't.
  • River and storm flooding needs a flood policy.
  • Manufactured homes are often insured by specialty insurers, so check your policy's water and flood terms.
  • Some Florida policies cap non-weather water losses, often at $10,000 per loss, through a limited water damage endorsement.

Who fixes a leak at my lot's water connection in a land-lease community?

Usually the park owner, if the connection is one the park is responsible for under its prospectus; Florida's Mobile Home Act requires park owners to keep those connections working. Leaks inside the home are usually yours. Tell the office and photograph where the water is coming from.

Do you work in manufactured-home and 55+ communities?

Yes. Let your community office know about the work if they require it, and have your lot number ready when you call.

Is Caloosahatchee or creek flooding covered by homeowners insurance?

No. Rising water from the river, a creek or storm surge is flood damage, handled by a flood policy.

Is my North Fort Myers home on county water or a well?

Check your water bill: Lee County Utilities or FGUA means a public system. No water bill usually means a private well, and often a septic tank too.

Who handles permits for water damage repairs in North Fort Myers?

Lee County's building department, because North Fort Myers is unincorporated. It also decides whether the 50% rule applies to a home in a flood zone.
