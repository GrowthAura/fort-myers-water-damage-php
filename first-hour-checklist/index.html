What to Do in the First Hour After Water Damage in Fort Myers

Water on the floor, dripping through the ceiling or backing up from a drain? Don't panic, and don't start tearing things out yet. What you do in the next hour decides how much of your home has to be replaced later. This checklist walks Fort Myers and Lee County homeowners through each step, in order. In the first hour after water damage: (1) make it safe by cutting power to wet areas, (2) stop the water at the fixture or main, (3) photograph everything, (4) move belongings out of the water, and (5) report the loss and start drying. Clean water can turn contaminated within 24 to 48 hours.

The First-Hour Checklist for Fort Myers Homes, Minute by Minute

Minutes 0 to 5: Make it safe

  • Turn off power at the breaker to any room where water touches outlets, cords, appliances or a light fixture. If the panel is wet or you'd have to stand in water to reach it, don't; call an electrician or your utility.
  • Get people and pets out from under any sagging or bulging ceiling.
  • Keep everyone away from sewage. Toilet, drain or floodwater is Category 3 and can make people sick.
  • Never use a household vacuum on water.

Minutes 5 to 10: Stop the water

  • Close the fixture valve under the sink or behind the toilet if that's the source.
  • Turn off the main shut-off if you can't find the source or the fixture valve won't close. In most Fort Myers slab homes it's where the line enters the house, often near the garage, or at the meter box near the street.
  • On a private well, common in Lehigh Acres, also turn off the well pump at its breaker.
  • For an AC leak, turn the system off at the thermostat.
  • For a water heater, close the cold-water valve on top and turn off its power or gas.
  • For a sewage backup, stop using all water in the house: no flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher.
  • For rain or a roof leak, you can't stop it from inside. Catch the water and protect what's below.

Minutes 10 to 20: Photograph and record

  • Take wide photos of every wet room, then close-ups of the water line on walls, stains, the source and damaged belongings.
  • Record a short video walking through the house.
  • Write down the time you found the water and what you saw.
  • Keep damaged items until your insurer says you can throw them out, or photograph them first if they must go.

Minutes 20 to 40: Protect your home and belongings

  • Move rugs, electronics, papers and small furniture out of the water.
  • Put foil or plastic under furniture legs so wood and fabric don't wick water.
  • Hang wet rugs and drapes away from walls.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks so air reaches the wet base.
  • Keep the AC running if it's safe and not the source; it pulls humidity down. Don't open windows on a humid Florida day; it usually adds moisture.

Minutes 40 to 60: Start drying and report it

  • Call for water removal and drying. Standing water and hidden moisture in walls, under tile and in the slab need commercial equipment and moisture readings.
  • Call a plumber, roofer or HVAC technician to fix the source if it isn't already fixed.
  • Report the loss to your insurer. Florida generally requires notice of a new property claim within one year of the date of loss, but reporting the same day is always better.
  • In a condo, tell building management and the unit above or below. See condo water damage.

Extra First-Hour Steps After a Storm or Flood in Lee County

Storm water brings risks a pipe leak doesn't. Add these to the checklist:

  • Smell gas? Leave. Get everyone out and call the gas company or 911 from outside.
  • Don't go back in until officials say it is safe, and never walk or drive through moving water.
  • Run generators outside only, well away from doors, windows and the garage.
  • Keep the power off if water reached outlets, the panel or appliances, until an electrician checks it.
  • Photograph the water line on inside and outside walls before anything is cleaned. It shows how high the water rose.
  • Follow any boil water notice from your utility before drinking or cooking with tap water.
  • Treat all floodwater as Category 3. Keep children and pets out of it, inside and in the yard.

What Not to Do After Water Damage in a Fort Myers Home

  • Don't walk into standing water with the power on.
  • Don't use a household vacuum or regular mop on large amounts of water or any sewage.
  • Don't turn on ceiling fans or lights in rooms with wet ceilings.
  • Don't tear out drywall or flooring before you photograph it.
  • Don't paint over stains or re-lay flooring until moisture readings confirm the area is dry.
  • Don't wait to see if it dries on its own. In Southwest Florida humidity, hidden water in walls and under tile rarely does before mold starts.
  • Don't sign anything you don't understand. Ask for a written scope before work beyond emergency water removal.

Before It Happens: Set Up Your Fort Myers Home in 15 Minutes

The first hour goes faster when you've done this once on a calm day.

Find your shut-offs

  • The main water valve, usually where the line enters the house or near the garage. Tag it so anyone can find it.
  • The meter box near the street, and whether you need a meter key to turn it.
  • The valves under each sink, behind each toilet and behind the washer. Turn each one to make sure it moves.
  • The cold-water valve on top of the water heater.
  • The well pump breaker, if your home is on a well.
  • Your breaker panel. Make sure each circuit is labeled.

Put together a small water damage kit

  • A flashlight and a phone charger or power bank.
  • Buckets, old towels, a roll of plastic sheeting and squares of foil for furniture legs.
  • Rubber gloves and boots.
  • A meter key, if your main is at the street.
  • A list of numbers: your insurer and policy number, a plumber, your HVAC company, condo management and 239-356-3708.

What to Have Ready When You Call for Help in Fort Myers

What Happens in the Next 1 to 5 Days in a Fort Myers Home

  • Day 1: water is extracted, moisture is mapped with meters and a thermal camera, materials that can't be saved are removed and drying equipment is set.
  • Days 2 to 4: moisture readings are taken daily and equipment is moved as areas dry.
  • Days 3 to 5 for most clean-water jobs: materials reach normal dry readings and equipment is removed. Slabs, hardwood and sewage jobs take longer.
  • After drying: repairs to drywall, baseboards, flooring and cabinets.

Insurance Steps in the First Hour for Florida Homeowners

This is general information, not advice about your policy.

  • Photograph before cleanup and keep damaged items or photos of them.
  • Take reasonable steps to stop further damage. Most policies expect it, and drying is one of those steps.
  • Report the claim promptly.
  • Keep every receipt for emergency work, supplies and any hotel stay.
  • Know the assignment-of-benefits rule. For Florida policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, you can't sign your insurance benefits over to a contractor after a loss.

Questions About the First Hour After Water Damage in Fort Myers

What is the first thing to do when you have water damage?

Make it safe: cut power to wet areas at the breaker and keep people away from sagging ceilings and sewage. Then stop the water at the fixture or main shut-off.

Should I call my insurance company or a restoration company first?

Stop the water first. Then start drying and report the claim as soon as you can. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, and waiting for an adjuster before drying usually makes the damage worse.

Should I turn off the electricity if there's water on the floor?

Yes, at the breaker for the affected rooms, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If the panel is wet, stay away and call an electrician or the power company.

Where is the main water shut-off in a Florida house?

Usually where the main line enters the house, often near the garage or an outside wall, or at the meter box near the street, which may need a meter key. Find it before you need it.

Do I need a meter key?

Only if your house valve is missing, stuck or hard to reach and you have to shut the water off at the meter box. A meter key is inexpensive and worth keeping with your flashlight.

Can I use fans and a wet vac myself?

For a small clean-water spill caught right away, yes. If water reached under flooring, into walls, under cabinets or came from a toilet or drain, it needs commercial drying and moisture readings.

Can I use a regular vacuum to suck up water?

No. A household vacuum isn't built for water and can shock you or burn out. A wet/dry shop vac works for small amounts.

Should I stay home tonight or go to a hotel?

Most families can stay after a clean-water leak. Leave if sewage or floodwater is inside, a ceiling is sagging, the power can't be made safe, or the only bathroom or kitchen can't be used. If your home isn't livable after a covered loss, many policies include additional living expenses, so keep hotel and meal receipts and ask your insurer what applies.

What should I do first after a hurricane or flood?

Wait until officials say it's safe, keep the power off if water reached outlets or the panel, run any generator outside only, and photograph the water line before cleanup. Treat all floodwater as Category 3.
