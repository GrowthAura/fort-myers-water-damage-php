Key facts about water damage in Estero

  • Much of Estero's housing is condos, coach homes and villas, where a leak in one home can reach others.
  • Many owners are away during the hottest, most humid months, so AC failures and slow leaks go unnoticed.
  • Interior AC closets are common, and a clogged condensate line soaks the closet, the hallway and the unit below.
  • Neighborhoods near the Estero River and other low-lying areas can flood in heavy rain and hurricanes.

Water Damage in Estero Right Now? Do This First

  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main. In a condo, also tell building management.
  • Cut power at your unit's breaker panel (in many condos it's in a hallway or entry closet) to rooms where water reaches outlets or appliances.
  • Water coming from above? Knock on the upstairs unit or call management to shut off its water.
  • Photograph and video everything before cleanup.
  • Arrange gate access for anyone coming out.
  • Start drying the same day, even if the association hasn't decided who pays yet.

Who Supplies Water and Sewer in Estero?

Estero doesn't have one utility. Depending on the neighborhood, water and sewer come from Lee County Utilities or from Bonita Springs Utilities, a member-owned, not-for-profit cooperative that also serves Bonita Springs and unincorporated south Lee County. Some older neighborhoods near the Estero River are still on septic, and the Village has a septic-to-sewer program for them. Your bill tells you who to call about a leak at the meter or a backup in the public line.

Condo or HOA? Why It Matters for Estero Coach Homes and Villas

Coach homes and villas can look alike and still be set up differently. Some are legally condominiums, where Florida Statute 718.111(11) sets the default split: the association's policy generally covers the building and common elements, and your HO-6 covers your interior finishes, improvements and belongings. Others are homes in a homeowners' association, where that condo statute doesn't apply and your declaration decides who insures the roof and outside walls. Your documents or management can tell you which you have.

Areas

Condo Leaks in Estero: Who Pays?

  • The association's master policy usually covers the building structure and common elements.
  • Your HO-6 policy usually covers your interior finishes, improvements and belongings.
  • The cause and your condo documents decide the details, and negligence can shift responsibility.

Read your declaration, talk to your insurer and start drying while it's sorted out.

Coach Homes, Villas and Shared Walls in Estero

Coach homes and villas often share a roof, walls or floors with a neighbor. A leak in one home can show up in the other first. If water appears on a shared wall or ceiling, contact your neighbor and the association quickly, shut off your own water if the source is unclear and photograph both sides if you can.

Areas

Returning to Estero for the Season

  • Check the AC first. A failed AC in summer means high humidity throughout the house.
  • Look and smell for musty odors, stains on ceilings and walls, and damp baseboards.
  • Check under sinks, around the water heater and behind the washer.
  • Photograph anything you find and note the date you discovered it.

Areas

Preventing Summer Damage in Estero Seasonal Homes

  • Shut off the main water and the water heater before you leave.
  • Set the AC to control humidity rather than turning it off.
  • Have the AC drain line cleared before summer.
  • Arrange regular checks by a home watch service, neighbor or property manager.
  • Install leak alarms or a smart shut-off valve that alerts your phone.
  • Leave interior doors open so air moves through the house.

Estero River Flooding

When rising water from the river or heavy rain reaches a home, it's floodwater, Category 3, even if it looks clear. Wet drywall, insulation, carpet and particle board usually come out before drying. Rising water needs a flood policy, not a standard homeowners policy, and new flood policies usually have a 30-day waiting period. When homes in a flood zone are rebuilt or substantially improved, the Village requires them to sit at least one foot above the base flood elevation.

Areas

Permits and the 50% Rule in Estero

The Village of Estero, not Lee County, issues building permits and makes 50% rule decisions inside the village. Substantial damage counts flood, wind, fire or other damage, so it can apply even when the repairs aren't flood-related. Expect to provide photos from before and after the damage and a market value, from an independent appraisal or the Lee County Property Appraiser.

Can someone get into my gated community?

Yes, with access arranged by you or your community. Have your gate information ready when you call.

Will my condo association handle the drying?

Sometimes the association handles common elements, but your unit's interior often falls to you and your HO-6. Ask management right away who handles what.

Is my coach home a condo or an HOA?

It depends on how your community was set up, not on how the homes look. Your declaration or management can tell you, and the answer decides whether Florida's condo insurance statute applies.

Does the 50% rule apply if the damage wasn't from a flood?

It can. The Village counts flood, wind, fire and other damage when deciding whether a home in a flood zone is substantially damaged.

Is Estero River flooding covered by my homeowners policy?

No. Rising water from the river or heavy rain is flood damage, handled by a flood policy.
