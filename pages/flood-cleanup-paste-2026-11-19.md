Key facts about flood cleanup in Lee County
• Floodwater from surge, canals, rivers and streets carries sewage, chemicals, fuel and debris.
• Drywall is usually cut about 2 or 4 feet up, or higher, based on moisture readings rather than the visible stain.
• New NFIP flood policies usually have a 30-day waiting period.
• Mold can start within 24 to 48 hours in Florida humidity, so muck-out and drying should start as soon as it's safe.
Before You Go Back Into a Flooded Fort Myers Home
• Wait for the all-clear from Lee County or your city before returning to a flooded area.
• Keep the power off. If water reached outlets or the panel, have an electrician check it before the power goes back on.
• Smell for gas. If you smell it, leave and call the gas company from outside.
• Never run a generator indoors or in a garage. Carbon monoxide kills.
• Wear rubber boots, gloves, long sleeves and an N95 mask.
• Watch for hazards: sagging ceilings, broken glass, nails and wildlife that came in with the water.
• Photograph and video everything before you move or throw anything away, including the water line on the walls.

Why Floodwater Cleanup in Fort Myers Is Different From a Leak
A burst pipe leaks clean water. Floodwater from storm surge, overflowing canals, rivers and street flooding picks up sewage, chemicals, fuel and debris on the way in. It is Category 3, the most contaminated type of water, and that changes the job:
• Porous materials it touched are removed, not dried in place.
• Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected before drying.
• Work is done in protective gear with the area contained.
• Drying comes after removal, not instead of it.
If the water came up through your drains rather than in from outside, see sewage backup cleanup (/sewage-cleanup/).
Saltwater Storm Surge in Fort Myers Homes
Surge on Fort Myers Beach, Sanibel, Pine Island, Iona and the Cape Coral canals is saltwater, and salt keeps working after the water leaves.
• Electrical. Electrical industry guidance says breakers, outlets, switches and GFCIs that sat in floodwater should generally be replaced, not dried and reused. Saltwater makes that even more important.
• Appliances and equipment. Water heaters, washers, dryers, refrigerators and AC condensers that sat in surge are often replaced; have them checked before use.
• Block, slab and framing. Salt residue is cleaned off during disinfection so it doesn't keep drawing moisture back into the wall.
• Metal parts. Hinges, fasteners and metal studs that took saltwater can corrode over time and should be looked at during the rebuild.
How Flood Cleanup Works in a Fort Myers Home, Step by Step
1. Water removal
Standing water is pumped and extracted. See emergency water removal (/water-removal/).
2. Muck-out
Wet carpet, pad, furniture and debris are removed and bagged or set out for curbside debris pickup.
3. Flood cuts
Drywall is cut in a straight line above the highest wet reading, and wet insulation behind it is removed.
4. Remove swollen materials
Particle board cabinets and vanities, baseboards, door casings and wet trim usually come out.
5. Clean and disinfect
The slab, studs, block and remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial.
6. Dry the structure
Dehumidifiers and air movers run until framing and slab read dry. See structural drying (/drying/).
7. Rebuild
Insulation, drywall, trim, cabinets and flooring go back once everything reads dry.

Septic Systems and Wells After a Lee County Flood
Parts of Lehigh Acres, North Fort Myers and other areas run on septic tanks and private wells, and both need care after a flood.
• Septic. Don't have the tank pumped while the ground is still saturated; an emptied tank can shift or float. Use as little water as you can until the drain field dries out.
• Wells. Don't drink well water that floodwater reached until the well is disinfected and the water is tested. The Florida Department of Health in Lee County can advise on testing.
• Drains backing up. If toilets and tubs gurgle or back up after the water goes down, the septic system or sewer line may be overloaded. See sewage backup cleanup (/sewage-cleanup/).

Flood Insurance Basics for Fort Myers Homeowners
This is general information, not advice about your policy.
• Homeowners doesn't cover flood. Rising surface water, storm surge and overflowing canals are covered by a flood policy from the NFIP or a private insurer.
• Waiting period. New NFIP policies usually have a 30-day waiting period, so coverage bought when a storm is forecast is usually too late.
• Wind vs water. Rain driven in through a storm-damaged roof or window is usually a homeowners claim; rising water is a flood claim.
• Proof of loss. NFIP policies generally ask for a signed proof of loss within 60 days of the flood, and FEMA sometimes extends that after large storms. Ask your adjuster for the date in writing.
• Below elevated homes. Areas below the lowest elevated floor often have limited flood coverage for finishes and contents.
• Citizens policyholders. Citizens home policies that include wind coverage now require a separate flood policy, reaching all of them on January 1, 2027.
• Document before you muck out. Photos, video, the water line on the walls and a list of damaged items.

Rebuilding After a Fort Myers Flood: Permits and the 50% Rule
• The 50% rule. In FEMA flood zones that start with A or V, Lee County building departments compare the repair total with the building's market value. At 50% or more, the building has to meet current flood rules, which can mean raising it. More on the 50% rule: water damage restoration in Fort Myers (/).
• ICC coverage. NFIP policies include ICC coverage that can pay up to $30,000 toward elevating, flood-proofing, relocating or demolishing a home the community declares substantially damaged. Request it within 60 days of the substantial damage notice.
• Permits. Ask your city or Lee County which repairs need a permit before the rebuild starts.
• Keep the paper trail. Moisture readings, drying logs, photos and repair invoices support both the permit review and the claim.
Renting Out or Selling a Fort Myers Home That Has Flooded
Florida now requires owners to tell the next tenant or buyer about past flooding.
• Landlords. Since October 1, 2025, a landlord signing a residential lease of one year or longer must give the tenant a separate flood disclosure form at or before signing (Florida Statutes 83.512). It asks whether the owner knows of past flooding and about past flood claims or flood-damage assistance. A tenant who doesn't receive it and suffers substantial flood loss may be able to end the lease and get prepaid rent back.
• Sellers. Sellers of residential property must give buyers a flood disclosure before the contract is signed (Florida Statutes 689.302). Since October 1, 2025, it covers flood-damage assistance received from any source, not only federal programs.
• Keep the records. Moisture readings, drying logs, photos and repair invoices from this cleanup are the clearest way to show later what flooded, what was replaced and when the structure was dry.
This is general information, not legal advice. Ask a real estate attorney or your property manager what applies to your lease or sale.

Questions About Flood Cleanup in Fort Myers
Is floodwater dangerous?
Yes. Floodwater is Category 3 water that can carry sewage, bacteria, chemicals and debris. Wear boots, gloves and a mask, and keep children and pets out of flooded areas.
How soon should I start flood cleanup?
As soon as officials say it's safe to return and the power is safe. Mold can start within 24 to 48 hours, so muck-out and drying should start right away.
Do I have to cut out drywall after a flood?
Usually yes. Drywall and insulation soaked by floodwater are removed above the wet line, typically with a 2-foot or 4-foot flood cut, so the wall cavity can be cleaned and dried.
Can outlets and breakers be reused after storm surge?
Generally no. Electrical parts that sat in floodwater, especially saltwater, are usually replaced. Have an electrician check the system before the power goes back on.
Does homeowners insurance cover storm surge?
No. Storm surge and rising water are flood damage, covered by a separate flood policy. Wind and wind-driven rain damage is usually a homeowners claim.
Will flood insurance help me raise my house?
It can. If the community declares your home substantially damaged, NFIP ICC coverage can pay up to $30,000 toward elevating or otherwise bringing it up to current flood rules. Ask your adjuster about it early.
Should I wait for the adjuster before cleaning up?
No. Photograph and video everything, keep small samples of carpet and drywall if you can, then start mucking out. Waiting lets mold grow, and policies expect you to prevent further damage.
Can I clean up flood damage myself?
You can remove wet belongings and carpet with proper protection. Removing drywall, disinfecting and drying the structure to measured readings is safer and more reliable with professional equipment.
How long does it take to dry a house after a flood?
After removal and disinfection, drying the slab and framing usually takes several days, often longer than a clean-water leak.
Can I stay in my house after a flood?
Only if the power, water and structure are safe. Many families stay elsewhere while wet materials are removed and the home is dried.
