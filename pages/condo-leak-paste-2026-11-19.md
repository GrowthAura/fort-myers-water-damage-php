Why Condo Leaks in Fort Myers Are Different From House Leaks
In a single-family home, a leak is your problem and your policy. In a condo, responsibility depends on where the water came from, what it damaged and what your declaration says. Water also travels: a washer hose that bursts on the fourth floor can wet the third, second and first floors through ceilings and wall cavities. That is why buildings in Fort Myers, Estero, Bonita Springs and Fort Myers Beach often have several units drying at once after a single failure.

Who Pays for a Condo Leak in Fort Myers? What Florida Law Says
Florida Statute 718.111(11) sets the default. The association's policy generally covers the building as originally installed: the structure and common elements such as the roof, exterior walls, shared pipes and original drywall.
The unit owner is generally responsible for items inside the unit, including:
• Floor, wall and ceiling coverings (tile, carpet, wood, paint, wallpaper)
• Electrical fixtures, appliances, water heaters and water filters
• Built-in cabinets and countertops
• Window treatments
• Personal property, plus any upgrades or improvements
Most owners insure these with an HO-6 policy.
When another owner caused it: if the leak came from a neighbor's unit, for example a washer hose or toilet line they failed to maintain, that owner or their insurer may be responsible for some of the damage. That usually depends on the cause and on negligence, and it is often settled between the insurers.

Who Should Start the Cleanup in a Fort Myers Condo?
Start drying right away, whoever ends up paying. Most policies expect owners to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and waiting while parties argue lets drywall, cabinets and flooring swell and grow mold.
In many buildings the association sends its own restoration vendor when several units or common elements are wet. You can usually still bring in a company for your own unit's finishes and belongings, but coordinate with management so two crews aren't working against each other. Ask:
• Who is drying the common elements and the shared walls?
• Who is responsible for my unit's flooring, cabinets and contents?
• Will moisture readings and photos be shared with me and my insurer?
Can the Association Get Into a Fort Myers Condo to Stop a Leak?
Usually, yes. Florida Statute 718.111(5) gives the association a right of access to each unit when it is needed for maintenance or repair of the common elements, or to prevent damage to the common elements or to another unit. That is how management can shut off water in a unit when the owner is away.
• Make sure management has a key or entry code for your unit, especially if you leave for the season.
• Give management a current phone number for you and anyone who checks the unit while you're gone.
• Your declaration and building rules set the details, such as notice and who may enter.

Questions About Condo Water Damage in Fort Myers
Who pays for water damage from the condo above me in Florida?
It depends on the source. Damage to the building structure or shared pipes is generally the association's; damage to your unit's finishes is generally yours under your HO-6 policy. If the owner above caused the leak, their insurer may be responsible for some of the damage. Your declaration and the insurers decide the details.
Should I call my insurance or the association first?
Call the unit above and building management first to stop the water. Then report the loss to your HO-6 insurer and start drying.
Can the association enter my unit to stop a leak?
Usually, yes. Florida law gives the association a right of access to units when needed to prevent damage to the common elements or to another unit. Keep a key or code on file with management.
Can I use my own restoration company in a condo?
Usually yes for your unit's interior, but coordinate with the association, especially when it has sent its own vendor for common elements or several units.
My unit is below and the upstairs owner isn't home. What now?
Call building management or the association. Many buildings can shut off a unit's water or the riser in an emergency. Keep documenting while you wait.
I rent my condo. Who do I call?
Tell your landlord and building management right away, then photograph your belongings. The owner's HO-6 and the association handle the building; your renters policy, if you have one, covers your belongings.
Does the association have to pay for my flooring and cabinets?
Generally no. Under Florida's condo law, floor coverings and built-in cabinets are usually the unit owner's to insure, unless your declaration says otherwise.
How long does condo drying take?
Most clean-water condo leaks dry in about 3 to 5 days per unit. Several units, sewage or wet concrete take longer.
What if the leak keeps coming back?
Repeat leaks from a common element, such as a roof, riser or association-maintained line, should be reported to the association in writing each time, with dates and photos.
