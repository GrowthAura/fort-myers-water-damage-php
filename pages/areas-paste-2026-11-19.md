Fort Myers
Fort Myers runs from the historic River District and older homes along McGregor Boulevard to Iona, Gateway, Whiskey Creek, San Carlos Park and newer communities off Daniels Parkway. Older homes may have cast iron drains and aging supply lines; condos and townhomes see leaks from units above; and neighborhoods along the Caloosahatchee River face surge and river flooding during storms. Most common: AC leak water damage (/ac-leak/), condo water damage (/condo-leak/) and cast iron pipe water damage (/cast-iron-pipe/). Full guide: water damage restoration in Fort Myers (/).
Cape Coral
Mostly single-family slab homes, many built between 1970 and 1999, laced with canals. City water, sewer and a separate irrigation system serve most of the Cape, while parts of the north still run on wells and septic. Slab leaks, original cast iron drains in older Cape neighborhoods, canal surge and leaks in seasonal homes are the most common calls. Full guide: water damage restoration in Cape Coral (/cape-coral/).
Lehigh Acres
A large, younger community of concrete block homes, many built in the 2000s and later. Many homes use private wells and septic tanks, heavy summer rain can flood streets and low lots, and air conditioners run hard most of the year. Full guide: water damage help in Lehigh Acres (/lehigh-acres/).
North Fort Myers
North of the Caloosahatchee, North Fort Myers mixes riverfront homes, golf communities and many manufactured-home communities. Soft floors from water heater and supply line leaks, water trapped in the belly wrap, roof leaks and river flooding are the most common problems. Full guide: water damage restoration in North Fort Myers (/north-fort-myers/). See also mobile home water damage (/mobile-home/).
Fort Myers Beach
Fort Myers Beach on Estero Island sits right on the Gulf, and many homes and businesses there have been rebuilt, often elevated. Storm surge, condo and vacation rental leaks and sewage problems after storms are the main concerns. Full guide: water damage help on Fort Myers Beach (/fort-myers-beach/).
Sanibel and Captiva
Many seasonal owners, condos and elevated homes. Hurricane surge is the biggest risk, followed by leaks that go unnoticed while owners are away. See water damage in vacant homes (/vacant-home/) and flood and storm cleanup (/flood-cleanup/).
Pine Island and Matlacha
Pine Island, Bokeelia, St. James City and Matlacha are low-lying and surrounded by water, so surge is a real threat. Many manufactured homes and seasonal residents mean floor and belly-wrap damage and leaks found after time away.
Estero
Gated communities, condos, coach homes and seasonal residents. The most common water damage is a leak from the unit above, an AC closet overflow, or a home found wet and musty when the owner returns. Full guide: water damage restoration in Estero (/estero/).
Bonita Springs
Condos along the coast and neighborhoods near the Imperial River. River and rain flooding, condo leaks and seasonal-home damage are the main concerns. Full guide: water damage restoration in Bonita Springs (/bonita-springs/).
San Carlos Park, Gateway and Iona in Lee County
These communities carry Fort Myers addresses but sit in unincorporated Lee County. San Carlos Park has single-family homes from several decades; Gateway is a planned community with mostly newer homes; Iona, near the Gulf and the Caloosahatchee, has canal-front and older homes that face storm surge.

Is water damage different on the coast than inland?
The drying process is the same, but the causes differ: surge and condos on the coast, wells, septic and street flooding inland, cast iron and slab leaks in older neighborhoods.
How soon can someone come out?
It depends on where you are, the time of day and how many calls are coming in. After a hurricane or major flood, waits across Lee County can be longer, so call early and start the first-hour steps while you wait.
Which parts of Lee County have the highest flood risk?
Barrier islands such as Fort Myers Beach, Sanibel, Captiva and Pine Island face the most storm surge, followed by canal-front homes in Cape Coral, Iona and areas along the Caloosahatchee and Imperial rivers.
My address says Fort Myers. Which building department handles my repairs?

What should I do while I wait for help?
