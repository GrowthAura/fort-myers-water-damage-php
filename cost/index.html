How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Fort Myers?

A wet closet and a flooded first floor are very different jobs, so there is no single price for water damage in Fort Myers. What you pay comes down to four things: the type of water, how many square feet got wet, what those materials are and how many days drying takes. This guide gives rough Fort Myers-area price ranges for a general idea, then shows what moves the number up or down in a Lee County home.

Key facts about water damage pricing

  • Water category is the biggest driver, because it decides how much has to be removed instead of dried.
  • Equipment is billed by the day, so every extra drying day adds to the bill.
  • Drying (mitigation) and rebuilding (restoration) usually come as two separate invoices.
  • Small jobs often cost more per square foot, because the trip, setup and monitoring visits carry minimums.
  • Your policy wording, not the contractor, decides how much insurance pays.

Why Water Damage Can Run Higher in a Fort Myers Home

  • Humidity. Fort Myers air stays humid most of the year, so wet materials rarely dry on their own and drying leans hard on dehumidifiers. Wetter air can mean more equipment or more days.
  • Block walls with furring strips. Interior drywall on concrete block usually sits on wood strips. Water wicks into the gap behind it, so baseboards come off and the bottom of the wall is often opened to dry the cavity.
  • Slab floors with vinyl plank. Water runs under floating floors along the slab. Plank is often lifted, and concrete gives up water slowly.
  • Attic air handlers and AC closets. An overflow in the attic soaks insulation and ceiling drywall, which usually come out. See AC leak water damage.
  • Condo buildings. One leak can reach two or three units, each with its own owner and insurer, plus building rules for access, elevators and work hours.
  • Saltwater storm surge. Surge water is Category 3, and salt left in walls and outlets adds cleaning and electrical checks.
  • Older drain lines. Cast iron failing under a slab adds plumbing repair, trenching and Category 3 cleanup. See cast iron pipe water damage.
  • Empty seasonal homes. A leak that ran for weeks means more tear-out, more drying days and often mold. See water damage in vacant homes.
  • After a big storm. Demand across Lee County climbs fast, and crews and equipment are stretched.
  • Access. Gated communities, high-rise condos, island homes and attics all add labor time.

How Fort Myers Water Damage Estimates Are Priced

Most insurers and many restoration companies write water damage estimates in Xactimate, estimating software that prices each line item from a price list set by ZIP code. Those price lists are updated monthly, so an estimate for a Fort Myers address reflects local labor and material prices for that month.

  • Ask for the estimate line by line, not as one lump sum.
  • When two estimates differ, compare the scope first: square footage, number of machines, drying days and what is being removed. That is usually where most of the gap sits.
  • Ask for the moisture readings and photos behind each line, so you and your adjuster can see why it is there.

What a Fair Fort Myers Water Damage Estimate Should Include

  • The water category and class
  • Square footage of each affected room
  • Extraction, by square foot or by the hour
  • Each machine, with the number of days it runs
  • Monitoring visits and daily moisture readings
  • Antimicrobial treatment, if it is used
  • Materials removed (carpet, pad, drywall, baseboards, cabinets) and disposal
  • Photos of the damage before anything is torn out
  • What is not included, such as rebuilding, plumbing or mold work

Warning signs in an estimate

  • One lump-sum number with no line items.
  • Equipment left running with no daily moisture readings.
  • A request to sign over your insurance benefits. For Florida policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, post-loss assignment of benefits isn't allowed.
  • Pressure to sign the same day for anything beyond emergency water removal.
  • Tear-out that the moisture readings don't support.

Costs Fort Myers Homeowners Often Forget

  • Fixing the cause. The plumber, roofer or HVAC technician who repairs the leak bills separately.
  • Matching flooring. Tile and vinyl plank often run through the whole house. Under Florida Statute 626.9744, when replaced items don't match in quality, color or size, a homeowners insurer must make reasonable repairs or replacement in adjoining areas, unless the policy says otherwise. Some policies now limit matching, so check yours.
  • Contents. Packing out, cleaning and storing furniture and belongings is priced separately, per item, box or hour.
  • Somewhere to stay. A hotel and meals if the home isn't livable; many policies include additional living expense.
  • Permits. Rebuilding drywall, electrical or plumbing can need a permit from your building department.
  • Your deductible. It comes off the claim payment first.

How Insurance Changes What You Pay in Fort Myers

This is general information, not advice about your policy. Your policy wording decides your coverage.

  • Deductible first. Leaks and appliance failures usually fall under your all other perils (AOP) deductible. Hurricane damage usually has its own deductible, often a percentage of dwelling coverage.
  • Sudden vs gradual. A sudden burst or overflow is often covered for the resulting damage; a slow leak that ran for months is often excluded.
  • Water damage caps. Some Florida policies carry a limited water damage endorsement that caps plumbing-leak losses, often at $10,000 per loss. On a $14,000 loss, the amount above the cap, plus your deductible, may be yours to pay.
  • Citizens policies. Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida's state-backed insurer, offers free emergency water removal through its own program for eligible non-weather water losses. Its published materials say that if you use your own mitigation contractor instead, emergency water mitigation is limited to $3,000, and permanent repairs outside its managed repair program can be capped at $10,000. Check your own policy before you hire.
  • Who pays whom. For Florida policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, you can't assign your benefits to a contractor after a loss. Your contract is with the restoration company, and insurance checks are often made out to you and sometimes your mortgage company too.
  • Flood is separate. Rising water and storm surge need a flood policy from the NFIP or a private insurer, not your homeowners policy.
  • Hotel and meals. Many policies include additional living expense when a covered loss makes your home unlivable. Keep every receipt.

Lee County's 50% Rule and Your Repair Total

If your home sits in a FEMA flood zone that starts with A or V, the repair total matters for more than the bill. Building departments in Lee County compare the price of repairing the building with its market value. At 50% or more, the building has to meet current flood rules, which can mean raising it.

  • Damage of any origin counts, not only floodwater.
  • Repairs from the same event are added together.
  • Work you do yourself still counts.

A clear, itemized scope shows the building department exactly what is being repaired. Ask the department that issues your permit before you plan the rebuild. More on the 50% rule: water damage restoration in Fort Myers.

How to Keep the Water Damage Bill Down in Fort Myers

  • Stop the water at the fixture or main shut-off right away.
  • Start drying the same day. Every day of delay adds tear-out and equipment days.
  • Move belongings off wet floors so contents don't add to the bill.
  • Photograph everything before tear-out; it can prevent a claim dispute later.
  • Ask for daily moisture readings, so equipment comes out as soon as the readings say dry.
  • Fix the cause properly: an AC float switch, new braided supply lines, a water heater pan.
  • Check your policy for water caps and insurer programs before you hire.

Is It Cheaper to Dry It Yourself in Fort Myers?

For a small clean-water spill on tile caught within minutes, yes. Towels, a wet vac and the AC running usually handle it. Renting a dehumidifier and air movers can work for a small area, but rental gear is only as good as the moisture readings behind it.

The catch in Fort Myers is hidden moisture. Without a moisture meter, water under vinyl plank, behind baseboards or inside cabinet bases is easy to miss, and in this humidity it turns into mold, swelling and a bigger bill later. Doing it yourself also leaves you without the readings and drying logs an insurer may ask for.

Questions About Water Damage Restoration Cost in Fort Myers

How much does water damage restoration cost in Fort Myers?

Most jobs in the Fort Myers area land roughly between $1,200 and $5,500. That is a rough idea, not an exact estimate or pricing. Every job is different, so call 239-356-3708 and ask about yours.

How much does it cost to dry out a house after a leak?

Rough Florida figures put structural drying at about $3 to $6 per square foot, with extraction on top when there is standing water. The real number depends on how far the water spread and how many days drying takes.

What is the price per square foot for water damage restoration?

As a rough area idea: extraction about $2 to $7 and drying about $3 to $6 per square foot, with local all-in examples around $10 to $11 per square foot and flood cleanup closer to $16 to $18. Clean water sits at the low end and sewage at the high end.

Are condo water damage jobs more expensive in Fort Myers?

Often, yes. South Florida cost guides put condo and high-rise losses about 20% to 40% higher than a house with the same damage, because water reaches more than one unit and building access, elevators and work hours add time.

Why can't I get an exact price over the phone?

The price depends on how far water spread inside walls and under flooring, and that only shows up with moisture meters and a thermal camera on site.

Does the restoration price include repairs?

Usually not. Water removal and drying are one invoice; drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint are another. Ask which parts are included before you sign.

Why did my estimate go up after the first day?

Moisture readings often show water spread farther than you could see, which adds square footage, removal or drying days. Ask for the moisture map and photos behind any change.

Is mold removal included in water damage restoration?

No. If mold has already grown, removal is separate work by a qualified Florida mold remediation company, with its own price.

Will I pay my deductible to the restoration company?

Often, yes. Insurance pays covered amounts above your deductible, so that part is usually yours, paid to the company directly or out of the claim payment.

What is the $3,000 water mitigation limit on some Florida policies?

Some policies, including Citizens policies for non-weather water losses, limit emergency water mitigation to $3,000 if you don't use the insurer's own water removal program. Check your declarations page or ask your agent before work starts.

Is it worth filing a claim for a small leak?

If the loss is close to your deductible, maybe not, since claims history can affect renewal. Fast drying still saves money either way by limiting tear-out.

Does the price differ in Cape Coral, Lehigh Acres or Estero?

The same drivers apply across Lee County: water category, square footage, materials and drying days. For a written number for your home in Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Lehigh Acres, Estero or Bonita Springs, call 239-356-3708.
