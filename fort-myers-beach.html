Key facts about water damage on Fort Myers Beach

  • Storm surge is the island's biggest water risk; it's saltwater mixed with sewage, sand and debris.
  • Many rebuilt homes are elevated, and flood coverage for ground-level enclosures is limited.
  • Vacation rentals and seasonal homes can sit empty, so leaks run unnoticed between guests.
  • Repairs in flood zones can trigger FEMA's substantial damage rule, often called the 50% rule.

Water Damage on Fort Myers Beach Right Now? Do This First

  • After a storm, wait for the all-clear from the Town of Fort Myers Beach or Lee County before returning.
  • Keep the power off if water reached outlets or the panel, until an electrician checks it.
  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main.
  • Photograph the water line inside and out, plus the ground-level enclosure, before anything is mucked out.
  • Rental owners: tell your property manager and the next guests if the unit isn't usable.
  • Condo owners: tell building management and the unit below.
  • Once re-entry is allowed and the power is safe, start removing wet materials the same day.

What Makes Water Damage Different on Fort Myers Beach?

  • Storm surge. Estero Island sits low between the Gulf and Estero Bay, so water can come from the beach side and the back bay. Porous materials surge touches usually have to go. See flood and storm cleanup.
  • Elevated rebuilds. Many homes and businesses now sit above ground-level parking, storage or entry areas that are built to flood.
  • Vacation rentals. Leaks from AC drains, water heaters, toilets and washers can sit between bookings.
  • Condos. Mid-rise and high-rise buildings mean a leak can travel to several units below. See condo water damage.
  • Salt air. Corrosion wears on fittings, water heaters and AC parts faster than it does inland.

Fort Myers Beach ZIP Code and Where the Town Ends

Fort Myers Beach is ZIP code 33931, covering Estero Island from the north end near the Matanzas Pass Bridge to the south end near Big Carlos Pass; 33932 is used for post office boxes. San Carlos Island, across Matanzas Pass, shares the Fort Myers Beach mailing address but isn't part of the Town, so permits and flood decisions there go through Lee County.

Who Supplies Water and Sewer on Fort Myers Beach?

Inside town limits, the Town of Fort Myers Beach runs the water lines and buys its treated water in bulk from Lee County Utilities. Lee County owns and runs the sewer system. That matters after a leak: a problem at the meter or in the street goes to the Town's utilities department, a backup in the public sewer goes to Lee County, and pipes inside your home are yours.

The 50% Rule on Fort Myers Beach

The Town of Fort Myers Beach decides when the 50% rule applies on Estero Island. If repairs reach half of the building's market value before the damage, the building has to be brought up to current floodplain standards, which often means elevation. The Town asks for a repair or improvement form listing the work on jobs of $2,500 or more. Keep detailed photos and invoices, and talk to the Town's building services staff before major work starts.

Ground-Level Enclosures Under Elevated Fort Myers Beach Homes

Many rebuilt island homes have ground-level enclosures for parking, storage and entry. These areas are built to flood, and NFIP flood coverage for an elevated, newer home in a high-risk zone is limited below the lowest elevated floor to a specific list of items, such as:

  • Foundation walls, anchorage and staircases attached to the building
  • Central air conditioners, water heaters and heat pumps, with the utility connections they need
  • As contents, clothes washers and dryers, and food freezers with the food in them

Finished walls, flooring and most belongings stored in the enclosure aren't on that list. Keep valuables and electronics out of these spaces, and read what your flood policy says about the area below the lowest elevated floor.

Condos on Fort Myers Beach

In island condos, a leak in one unit can reach several floors below. Florida Statute 718.111(11) generally puts the building and common elements on the association's policy and the finishes inside each unit on the owner's HO-6. Ask the association how the building's flood policy treats ground-level areas, and report every leak to management right away. See condo water damage.

Vacation Rental Water Damage on Fort Myers Beach

  • Have your manager or cleaner check for water, smells and AC problems at every turnover.
  • Keep the AC on and set to control humidity between bookings.
  • Install leak alarms or automatic shut-off valves where practical.
  • Document everything before cleanup if you need to file a claim.

After a Storm: Getting Back to Fort Myers Beach

  • Follow official re-entry rules from the Town of Fort Myers Beach and Lee County.
  • Bring boots, gloves, N95 masks, a flashlight and a phone charger.
  • Don't turn the power on until an electrician says the panel and wiring are safe.
  • Photograph everything first, including the water line, before mucking out.
  • Keep receipts for supplies, hotels and emergency work.
  • Call early; demand across the island and the county is high after a major storm.

Questions About Water Damage on Fort Myers Beach

Is there 24-hour water damage help on Fort Myers Beach?

Yes. Call 239-356-3708, answered 24/7, for water damage anywhere on Fort Myers Beach and San Carlos Island.

Does flood insurance cover the enclosure under my elevated home?

Only partly. For an elevated, newer home in a high-risk flood zone, NFIP coverage below the lowest elevated floor is limited to a list of items such as the foundation, attached stairs, central AC, water heaters, washers, dryers and food freezers. Finished walls, flooring and most stored belongings aren't covered there.

What is the 50% rule on Fort Myers Beach?

If repairs to a damaged building in a flood zone reach 50% or more of its market value, it usually has to meet current flood rules, often meaning elevation. The Town makes that decision and asks for a repair or improvement form on jobs of $2,500 or more.

Is San Carlos Island part of the Town of Fort Myers Beach?

No. San Carlos Island uses a Fort Myers Beach address, but it's in unincorporated Lee County, so permits and flood decisions go through Lee County, not the Town.

Who handles a sewer backup on Fort Myers Beach?

Lee County runs the public sewer, so a backup in the main line goes to Lee County Utilities. Drain lines inside your home are yours. Either way, sewage inside is Category 3 and needs cleanup. See sewage backup cleanup.

My rental guests found water in the unit. What now?

Shut off the water, move guests if the unit isn't safe or usable, photograph everything and call. Let your manager and insurer know.

Can I go back to the island right after a hurricane?

Only after officials allow re-entry. Keep the power off until an electrician checks it, and wear protective gear around floodwater.

How do I protect my rental from leaks between guests?

Have the cleaner check under sinks, around the water heater and in the AC closet at each turnover, keep the AC running and consider leak alarms or an automatic shut-off valve.
