Key facts about AC leaks in Fort Myers

  • Your AC pulls water out of the air as it cools. In humid Southwest Florida that is a lot of water every day, and it all has to leave through a small PVC drain line.
  • Attic air handlers leak into ceilings; closet units leak into floors, baseboards and the next room; condo units often leak into the unit below.
  • AC condensate caught quickly is usually treated as clean water, but water that sits for a day or two can become Category 2.
  • An HVAC company fixes the AC. Drying the ceiling, walls and floor is water damage restoration work.

AC Leaking in Your Fort Myers Home Right Now? Do This First

  • Turn the AC off at the thermostat. This stops new condensate from forming. If water is near the air handler's electrical parts, switch off its breaker too.
  • Cut power to wet fixtures. If water drips from a light, ceiling fan or smoke detector, turn off that circuit at the breaker and stay clear.
  • Catch the water with buckets and towels, and move furniture, rugs and electronics away.
  • Check the ceiling. If it sags or bulges, keep everyone out from underneath it. See what to do about a leaking ceiling.
  • Look at the air handler. In a closet or garage unit, check whether the drain pan is full. Don't climb into the attic if the ceiling below is soft.
  • Photograph the stain, the drip, the pan and the wet floor before cleanup.

Why Is My AC Leaking Water Inside My Fort Myers Home?

Your air conditioner removes moisture as it cools. That water collects on the evaporator coil, drips into a drain pan and leaves the house through a condensate drain line. When any part of that path fails, the water ends up in your home instead. The most common causes:

  • Clogged condensate drain line. Algae, slime and dust build up inside the line. This is the most common reason AC units leak indoors in Florida.
  • Full, rusted or cracked drain pan. Older metal pans rust through; plastic pans crack.
  • Failed float switch. This safety switch should shut the AC off when the pan fills. If it is missing, stuck or wired wrong, the pan overflows.
  • Frozen evaporator coil. A dirty filter or low refrigerant can freeze the coil. When it thaws, more water comes off than the pan can handle.
  • Disconnected or sagging drain line. A joint pulls apart in the attic or wall and drips onto drywall.
  • Condensate pump failure. Units that pump water out, common in condos and interior closets, leak when the pump stops.
  • Sweating ducts and vents. Cold supply ducts in a hot, humid attic collect condensation that drips through ceiling vents.

The Backup Drain and Float Switch in Fort Myers AC Systems

Florida's mechanical code calls for a backup wherever an AC overflow could damage the building, such as an attic or a closet over finished floors. That backup is usually one of these:

  • A secondary drain pan under the air handler, with its own drain line. In many Fort Myers homes that line ends over a window or under the eave, so dripping water there is a warning sign.
  • A float switch in the pan or drain line that shuts the AC off when water rises.

If your AC has neither, or the float switch has never been tested, ask your HVAC company to check it at the next service. It is a small part that prevents a large ceiling repair.

Signs of AC Water Damage in Fort Myers Homes

AC leaks are often slow and can run for days before anyone sees water, especially with attic units. Watch for:

  • Brown or yellow stains on the ceiling near the air handler or a supply vent.
  • Bubbling paint or soft drywall in the hallway beside the AC closet.
  • Swollen or stained baseboards next to the air handler.
  • Damp carpet near the closet, or vinyl plank lifting at the edges.
  • A musty smell when the AC runs.
  • Rust on the bottom of the air handler cabinet, or a pan that is always wet.
  • The AC shutting off on its own, which can mean the float switch tripped.

AC Repair vs. Water Damage Restoration in Fort Myers: Who Fixes What?

An HVAC technician fixes the cause: clears the drain line, replaces the pan, installs or resets the float switch, or repairs a frozen coil. That stops new water.

They don't dry your house. Water already in the ceiling, walls, insulation and floor stays there unless it is measured and dried. Water damage restoration covers the rest:

  • Moisture mapping with meters and a thermal camera to find every wet area, including inside walls and above the ceiling.
  • Removing what can't be dried, such as soaked insulation, sagging drywall and swollen baseboards.
  • Drying with dehumidifiers and air movers, with readings logged daily. See how structural drying works.
  • Photos and moisture logs for your records and your insurer.

The best order is the HVAC company first to stop the water, then drying the same day. If the HVAC company can't come until tomorrow, drying can still start as long as the AC stays off.

How AC Leak Damage Is Dried and Repaired in Fort Myers

  • Ceiling: a small stain from a short leak can often be dried in place. A sagging, soft or stained-through ceiling is usually cut out and replaced, and wet insulation above it is removed.
  • Walls: drywall next to an air handler closet often wicks water up from the floor. It can usually be dried in place if caught early; if it stayed wet for days, the bottom of the wall may be cut out.
  • Floors: tile on slab usually stays and is dried. Luxury vinyl plank often has to be lifted because it traps water on the concrete. Carpet pad near the closet is usually replaced. See water damaged floors.
  • Closet: the air handler platform, closet baseboards and any particle board nearby are checked for swelling.
  • Attic insulation: wet blown-in insulation is pulled back or removed so the ceiling drywall can dry from above, then replaced once the ceiling reads dry.

Most clean-water AC leaks dry in about 3 to 5 days once drying starts. Leaks that ran for weeks take longer and usually mean more tear-out.

Is AC Condensate Water Clean in a Fort Myers Home?

AC condensate is usually treated as Category 1, clean water, when it is caught quickly. But the drain pan and line can hold algae and bacteria, and water that sits for a day or two in Fort Myers humidity can become Category 2. If the leak ran for days, plan on antimicrobial treatment and more material removal.

Does Insurance Cover AC Leak Water Damage in Florida?

This is general information, not advice about your policy. It depends on how the leak happened. Florida policies usually separate sudden damage from slow, long-term leaks:

  • Often covered: water damage from a sudden overflow, such as a drain line that clogs and spills, a pan that cracks or a coil that thaws all at once.
  • Often excluded: damage from a leak that ran for weeks, which insurers may treat as gradual seepage or lack of maintenance.
  • Usually not covered: repairing the AC unit itself. The policy typically addresses the resulting water damage, not the equipment.
  • Mold coverage is often capped.

Report the date you found the leak truthfully, photograph everything and keep the HVAC technician's invoice, since it shows the cause. Read how insurance handles water damage in Florida.

How to Stop AC Leaks in Your Fort Myers Home

  • Service the drain line. Have the condensate line flushed and checked at least once a year; many Florida homeowners do it twice because algae grows fast here. Ask your HVAC company whether a vinegar flush between visits suits your system.
  • Change the filter on schedule. A clogged filter can freeze the coil.
  • Test the float switch. Make sure a working float switch is installed and that it actually shuts the system off.
  • Check the secondary pan. Attic units should sit over a secondary drain pan that is clean, dry and not rusted.
  • Look once a month. Open the air handler closet and check for water in the pan or rust on the cabinet.
  • Watch the backup line. If water drips from the pipe over a window or under the eave, call the HVAC company the same day.
  • Add a leak sensor. A small sensor in the pan or closet sends an alert to your phone when water appears.
  • Plan for the season. If you leave for the summer, have someone check the AC regularly. See water damage in vacant homes.

Questions About AC Leak Water Damage in Fort Myers

Why is my AC leaking water inside?

Most often because the condensate drain line is clogged, so water backs up and overflows the drain pan. Other causes include a cracked pan, a failed float switch, a frozen coil or a disconnected drain line.

Can I keep running my AC if it's leaking?

No. Turn it off until the cause is fixed. Running it keeps producing water that ends up in your ceiling or walls.

Why is water dripping from a pipe over my window?

That pipe is usually the end of your AC's backup drain line. Water there means the main drain line is clogged and the backup is doing its job. Call the HVAC company before the backup clogs too.

My AC shut off by itself and the pan is full. What happened?

The float switch tripped, which means it worked. The drain line is still clogged, so leave the AC off until it is cleared, then check the ceiling and floor near the unit for water that got out before the switch tripped.

Is a small ceiling stain from the AC an emergency?

Not always, but it means water has already soaked through insulation and drywall. Have it checked within a day or two, because hidden moisture in Florida humidity can lead to mold within 24 to 48 hours.

Will the HVAC company fix my ceiling?

Usually not. HVAC companies fix the air conditioner. Drying and repairing the ceiling, walls and floor is water damage restoration work.

How long does it take to dry after an AC leak?

Most clean-water AC leaks dry in about 3 to 5 days with dehumidifiers and air movers. Longer leaks and wet slabs take more time.

How often should the AC drain line be cleaned in Fort Myers?

Have it cleared at every AC service, which in Fort Myers usually means at least once a year and often twice, because the system runs most of the year. Between visits, many HVAC companies suggest pouring a cup of white vinegar into the drain line's access port every few months to slow algae growth. Ask your HVAC company what suits your system.

Why does my AC closet smell musty?

A musty smell usually means water has been sitting in the pan, the closet floor or the wall behind the unit. Check for a wet pan or rust, and have the area measured for hidden moisture.

Who pays when my condo AC leaks into the unit below?

It depends on your condo documents and where the leak started. In most condos the AC serving only your unit is the owner's responsibility, so the claim often starts with your HO-6 policy. Tell building management and your insurer right away.
