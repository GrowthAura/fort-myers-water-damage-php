Key facts about water damage in Lehigh Acres

  • Many homes run on private wells, which add pumps, pressure tanks and treatment equipment that can leak.
  • A well pump keeps pushing water into a broken line until the pump is shut off at its breaker.
  • Heavy summer storms can flood streets and low lots; water from outside is floodwater (Category 3).
  • Lehigh Acres has many rentals, so landlords and tenants should agree quickly on who calls for drying.

Water Damage in Your Lehigh Acres Home Right Now? Do This First

  • Shut off the water. On a well, turn off the well pump at its breaker as well as the main valve.
  • Cut power at the breaker to wet rooms, and to the well pump if a line is leaking.
  • Turn off the AC at the thermostat if it's leaking.
  • On septic, stop using water if toilets or tubs are backing up.
  • Photograph everything, including the pressure tank and well equipment if they're involved.
  • Start drying the same day. Mold can start within 24 to 48 hours in Florida humidity.

What Makes Water Damage Different in Lehigh Acres Homes

  • Block homes on slabs. Water runs under flooring and wicks up the drywall inside block walls, into the gap at the furring strips.
  • Private wells. A pump, a pressure tank and supply lines that can fail, often in the garage or a utility closet.
  • Hard well water. Well water in the area tends to be hard, so water heaters, valves and fittings scale up and can fail sooner.
  • Septic tanks. Heavy rain can saturate the drain field and push water back into the house.
  • Drainage during summer storms. Some streets and low lots flood deeply in heavy rain.
  • AC that rarely rests. Inland Lehigh Acres runs hot, and clogged condensate lines are a top cause of indoor water damage.

Storm and Street Flooding in Lehigh Acres

Heavy rain can leave some streets under deep water. When water from outside reaches a garage or living area, it's floodwater, Category 3, even if it looks clear.

  • Wet drywall, insulation and carpet usually come out before drying.
  • The damage is covered by a flood policy, not standard homeowners insurance.
  • Canal and drainage problems go to LAMSID, which took over stormwater from the old East County Water Control District.
  • Lehigh Acres is inland, so storm surge is far less of a risk than on the coast, but hurricane rain can still flood low-lying homes.
  • In FEMA flood zones that start with A or V, Lee County applies the 50% rule when you rebuild.

Lehigh Acres Rental Homes: Landlords and Tenants

  • Tenants. Stop the water if you safely can, photograph everything and tell your landlord or property manager right away. Renters insurance usually covers your belongings, not the building.
  • Landlords and property managers. Florida law generally requires landlords to keep plumbing in reasonable working condition (Florida Statutes 83.51), and the building is usually your insurance claim. Fast drying protects the property and keeps the home livable.
  • Both. Agree on who calls for drying, and keep copies of photos and messages.

Insurance for Lehigh Acres Water Damage

This is general information, not advice about your policy.

  • Sudden leaks such as burst lines, appliance failures and AC overflows are often covered for the resulting damage.
  • Slow leaks that ran for weeks are often excluded.
  • Flooding from outside needs a flood policy.
  • Septic and sewer backups usually need a water backup endorsement.
  • Renters: the landlord's policy usually covers the building and renters insurance covers belongings.

Questions About Water Damage in Lehigh Acres

Is there 24-hour water damage help in Lehigh Acres?

Yes. Call 239-356-3708, answered 24/7, for water damage anywhere in Lehigh Acres.

My home is on a well. How do I shut the water off?

Turn off the well pump at its breaker and close the main valve near the pressure tank. That stops the pump from pushing more water into a broken line.

My well pressure tank is leaking into the garage. What do I do?

Turn off the pump at its breaker, close the valve near the tank and photograph the leak. Have a well service company repair the tank, and have the garage walls and floor checked for moisture.

My septic is backing up after heavy rain. What now?

Stop using water, keep people and pets away from the overflow and photograph it. Don't have the tank pumped while the ground is still flooded. Call a septic company, and treat anything the backup touched as sewage.

Street flooding got into my garage. Is that covered?

Water rising from outside is flood damage, covered by a flood policy rather than standard homeowners insurance. Photograph everything before cleanup.

Who do I call about flooded canals or street drainage?

Canal and stormwater drainage in Lehigh Acres is handled by the Lehigh Acres Municipal Services Improvement District (LAMSID).

My AC closet floor is wet. What should I do?

Turn the AC off at the thermostat, soak up standing water and check whether the drain pan is full. Then have the drain line cleared and the closet walls and floor checked for hidden moisture.

I rent my Lehigh Acres home. Who pays for water damage?

The landlord usually handles the building and its repairs, and renters insurance usually covers your belongings. Tell your landlord right away and photograph everything.

How long does drying take in a Lehigh Acres home?

Most clean-water leaks dry in about 3 to 5 days. Floodwater, sewage and wet concrete under flooring take longer.
