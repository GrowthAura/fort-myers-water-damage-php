Key facts about water damage in seasonal homes

  • The usual culprits are AC failure or condensate overflow, water heater leaks, supply line failures and roof leaks from summer storms.
  • Southwest Florida's empty season, roughly April to October, overlaps hurricane season and peak humidity.
  • Many policies have vacancy or unoccupancy wording. Home watch reports and utility records help show when damage began.
  • Clean water left for days can become Category 2, and mold is more likely.

Found Water Damage After Time Away From Fort Myers? Do This First

  • Shut off the main water if you find active water or don't know the source.
  • Check for safety. If water is near outlets or the electrical panel, or a ceiling sags, stay out of that area and cut power at the breaker if it's safe to reach.
  • Photograph and video everything before touching it: wet floors, stains, mold, the water heater, under sinks, the AC closet and the thermostat reading.
  • Write down the date and time you found it, and when the house was last visited or checked.
  • Don't open anything up yet. Leave baseboards and drywall in place until they are photographed.
  • Report the loss to your insurer and get drying started. Waiting makes the damage, and the claim, harder.

What Usually Fails While a Fort Myers Home Sits Empty

  • AC failure or power loss. If the AC stops from a breakdown or storm outage, indoor humidity climbs and mold can grow on walls, closets and furniture within days.
  • AC condensate overflow. The AC keeps running but the drain line clogs, and water spills into the closet, ceiling or floor for weeks. See AC leak water damage.
  • Water heater leaks. Tanks rust through at the bottom and leak slowly, or fail all at once in a garage or closet. See water heater leak cleanup.
  • Supply line failures. Braided lines to toilets, sinks, washers and ice makers split without warning. See burst pipe water damage.
  • Roof and window leaks from summer downpours and tropical storms, often showing up as ceiling stains. See ceiling leak water damage.
  • Chewed lines. Rodents in quiet houses sometimes chew plastic dishwasher, ice maker or washer lines.
  • Leaks from the unit above in condos, where your neighbor may also be away. See condo water damage.

Is That Musty Smell in Your Fort Myers Home Humidity or Water Damage?

A closed-up house in Southwest Florida can smell musty from humidity alone, but the smell can also come from a hidden leak or mold behind walls.

  • Check indoor humidity with an inexpensive hygrometer. Indoor relative humidity is generally best kept below about 60 percent; readings well above that mean the AC wasn't keeping up.
  • Look for visible mold on walls, ceilings, closet contents and the AC return grille.
  • Feel the bottoms of walls and baseboards near bathrooms, the kitchen and the AC closet for dampness or softness.
  • Watch the water meter. With every fixture off, a moving meter means a leak somewhere.
  • Smell the drains. A sewer smell after months away often means the water in the drain traps has dried up. Run water in every sink, tub, shower and floor drain, and flush each toilet, to refill them.

If you see mold on the air handler or vents, have the AC checked before running it hard, so it doesn't spread spores through the ducts.

How Long-Running Leaks Change the Cleanup in a Fort Myers Home

  • Water category drifts. Clean water left for 24 to 48 hours or more can become Category 2, and long-standing water is often treated as more contaminated.
  • More material comes out. Drywall, baseboards, cabinet bases and vinyl plank that stayed wet for weeks usually can't be saved.
  • Slabs hold more water. Concrete under tile and plank soaks up water and takes longer to dry.
  • Mold is more likely. If mold has grown, bring in a qualified Florida mold remediation company to remove it.

Insurance When Nobody Was Home: What Fort Myers Owners Should Know

This is general information, not advice about your policy.

  • Vacancy and unoccupancy clauses. Many policies limit coverage for certain losses after a home is vacant or unoccupied for a set number of days. Read yours or ask your agent.
  • Sudden vs. gradual. A supply line that burst is treated differently from a slow leak that ran for months. Report the date you found it honestly.
  • Proof of care. Home watch reports, utility bills showing normal water use and smart thermostat or leak-sensor logs help show the home was checked and when the damage began.
  • Mold limits. Many Florida policies cap mold coverage.
  • Tell it plainly. Tell the adjuster when you left, how the house was checked and when you found the damage. Don't guess at the cause; let the inspection show it.

What a Good Home Watch Visit Covers in Lee County

Home watch can't stop a pipe from failing, but a thorough visit catches a leak in days instead of months. Ask for visits that include:

  • A walk through every room, with dated photos sent to you.
  • The AC: is it running, is the drain pan dry, and what do the thermostat and humidity read?
  • Ceilings under the attic AC and bathrooms, and along exterior walls.
  • Under every sink, around toilets, behind the washer and at the water heater.
  • Running water in each sink and tub and flushing each toilet so drain traps don't dry out.
  • A check of doors, windows and the roof line after every major storm, as soon as it's safe.
  • A written report you can keep for your insurer.

How to Protect Your Fort Myers Home Next Time You Leave

  • Shut off the main water before you go, and turn off the water heater at its breaker or set it to vacation.
  • Leave the AC running and set to control humidity rather than turning it off. Ask your HVAC company for the right setting for your system.
  • Have the AC drain line flushed before you leave.
  • Arrange home watch visits on a schedule, with written reports and photos, and a visit after every major storm.
  • Install leak sensors or an automatic water shut-off valve that alerts your phone.
  • Replace old braided supply lines and washer hoses before they fail.
  • In a condo, leave a key or code with management and check your building's rules for owners who are away.

If a Storm Hits Lee County While You're Away

  • Ask your home watch or a neighbor to check the house as soon as officials say it's safe, and to send photos of every room and the outside.
  • Power outages stop the AC. Once power is back, have someone confirm the AC restarted and the humidity is coming down.
  • Wind-driven rain often shows up days later as a ceiling stain or wet carpet along a window wall. Ask for a second check a few days after the storm.
  • Report any damage to your insurer as soon as you know about it, even before you can travel back.

Questions About Water Damage in Vacant Fort Myers Homes

I came back to a wet floor. Can I just dry it with fans?

Only if it's a small, recent spill on a hard floor. If water sat for days or reached under flooring, cabinets or walls, it needs moisture mapping and commercial drying, or it will keep feeding mold.

Will insurance cover water damage if my house was empty?

It depends on your policy's vacancy or unoccupancy wording and whether the leak was sudden or gradual. Read the policy and document everything before cleanup.

The AC was off when I got back and there's mold. What now?

Don't run the AC hard until it's checked, keep windows closed and photograph the mold. Bring in a qualified Florida mold remediation company for removal. Drying and humidity control stop it from getting worse.

Why does my house smell like sewer after months away?

Usually the water in the drain traps has dried up and sewer gas is coming through. Run water in every sink, tub, shower and floor drain and flush each toilet. If the smell stays, have a plumber check the line.

How long does drying take after a long leak?

Clean-water drying often takes about 3 to 5 days, but long-running leaks usually need tear-out first and more time on concrete slabs.

Can home watch prevent this?

It can't stop a pipe from failing, but regular visits catch leaks early, and documented visits help with insurance questions.

What if a hurricane hits while I'm away?

Have someone check the house as soon as it's safe and again a few days later, confirm the AC restarted after the power came back, and report any damage to your insurer right away.

What's the first thing to check when I get back?

Smell the air, then check the AC closet, under the kitchen sink, around toilets and at the water heater. That is where most leaks in empty homes start.
