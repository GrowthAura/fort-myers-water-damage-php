Key facts about water removal in Fort Myers

  • Extraction removes the liquid water. Structural drying removes the water that has already soaked into drywall, slab, baseboards and cabinets.
  • Clean water can turn into contaminated Category 2 water within about 24 to 48 hours, and mold can start growing in the same window in Florida humidity.
  • Most Lee County homes sit on concrete slabs, so water spreads sideways under flooring instead of draining away.
  • Water from a toilet, drain or flood is Category 3 and is handled with protective gear and removal of porous materials.

Water Spreading in Your Fort Myers Home Right Now? Do This First

  • Cut the power. Switch off the breaker to any room where water reaches outlets, cords or appliances. Never stand in water to reach the panel.
  • Stop the source. Close the valve behind the toilet, sink or washer, or shut the main valve, usually where the line enters the house, often in the garage. For an AC overflow, turn the system off at the thermostat.
  • Photograph everything. Take photos and video of the water, the source and every wet room before anything is moved.
  • Lift what you can. Get rugs, electronics, papers, photos and small furniture out of the water. Put furniture legs on foil or plastic lids.
  • Skip the household vacuum. It isn't built for water and can shock you.
  • Stay out of dirty water. If the water came from a toilet, a drain or outside, keep out of it and keep children and pets away.

What Happens During Emergency Water Removal in Fort Myers?

Emergency water removal follows the same order on almost every job: make it safe, find the water, take it out, remove what can't be saved and set up drying.

  1. 1

    Safety and source check

    The water source is confirmed stopped, power to wet areas is confirmed off and the water category is identified: clean (Category 1), gray (Category 2) or black (Category 3).

  2. 2

    Moisture mapping

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show how far the water traveled under tile and vinyl plank, behind baseboards, under cabinets and up into the bottom of the drywall. The wet area is almost always bigger than the puddle.

  3. 3

    Water extraction

    Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water and the water held in carpet. Weighted extraction tools press out water that a wand alone leaves behind in carpet and pad. Submersible pumps handle deep water in garages or after storms.

  4. 4

    Removal of what can't be saved

    Soaked carpet pad, swollen particle board, wet insulation and anything touched by sewage are usually removed, photographed and listed for your records and your insurer.

  5. 5

    Drying setup

    Dehumidifiers and air movers are placed and the first moisture readings are logged. From here the job becomes structural drying, which runs until the materials reach normal dry readings.

How Fast Does Water Need to Come Out of a Fort Myers Home?

The sooner, the better. Every hour water sits, it wicks higher into drywall and deeper under flooring, and more material has to be removed later.

What Equipment Is Used for Water Extraction in Fort Myers Homes?

  • Truck-mounted extractors: the strongest suction, used for large areas and deep carpet in single-family homes.
  • Portable extractors: for condos, upper floors, gated communities and tight spaces a truck hose can't reach.
  • Submersible pumps: for deep standing water in garages, lanais or ground floors after heavy rain.
  • Weighted extraction tools: press water out of carpet and pad.
  • Moisture meters and thermal cameras: find water you can't see behind walls and under floors.
  • Dehumidifiers and air movers: start pulling moisture out of the air and the structure once the liquid water is gone.

The more water extraction removes, the shorter the drying stage and the less tear-out the house needs.

What You Get After Water Removal in Fort Myers

Can You Remove the Water Yourself With a Shop Vac in Fort Myers?

Sometimes. A wet vac, towels and fans can handle a small clean-water spill on tile that you caught right away. It usually isn't enough when:

  • The water sat for more than a few hours or reached more than one room.
  • It ran under tile, vinyl plank, laminate, cabinets or baseboards.
  • The water came from a toilet, a drain, a sewer line or floodwater.
  • Drywall feels soft, paint is bubbling or baseboards are swelling.
  • You notice a musty smell a day or two later.

The water you can see is rarely the problem. The water inside walls and under flooring is what turns into swelling and mold later.

What Happens to Furniture and Belongings During Water Removal in Fort Myers?

Contents are handled alongside the water, not after it. On most jobs:

  • Furniture is lifted onto blocks or foil tabs, or moved to a dry room, so the legs don't wick water or stain the carpet.
  • The most fragile things move first. Rugs, boxes, papers, photos and electronics are moved before anything else, because they are the most likely to be ruined by sitting water.
  • Everything is photographed and listed before anything is thrown away, so your insurer can see what was damaged. Keep a sample of the carpet and pad if your adjuster hasn't seen them.
  • Pack-outs for bigger jobs. When several rooms are affected or repairs will take weeks, contents may be boxed, listed and stored off-site, then brought back once the rooms are rebuilt.
  • Sewage and floodwater change the rules. Porous items such as mattresses, upholstered furniture and stuffed toys usually can't be made safe and are listed for disposal; hard items can often be cleaned and disinfected.
  • Wet papers and photos can sometimes be saved by freezing them until they can be dried properly. Don't pull apart stuck photos while they are wet.

Ask what will be moved, stored or thrown out before it happens, and keep your own list.

Water Removal in Fort Myers Block Homes, Condos, Garages and Lanais

Concrete block homes

Drywall in most Fort Myers homes is fastened to wood furring strips over concrete block. Water that gets behind the baseboard sits in that gap, against the block, where an extractor can't reach it. Pulling the baseboard and checking the cavity with a meter is part of the first visit.

Tile on slab

Tile usually stays, but water works its way under loose or hollow-sounding tiles and into the grout. Extraction takes what is on top; the slab underneath is dried and checked with readings over the following days.

Condos

Upper-floor units usually need portable equipment, and the water may already be in the unit below. Tell building management right away so a shared riser or the unit above can be shut off, and photograph both units.

Garages

Water heaters and washers often sit in the garage. Water on a garage slab runs under the door into the house and soaks the bottom of the shared wall and anything stored on the floor.

Lanais and sliding doors

During heavy summer storms, wind-driven rain can push under sliders and lanai thresholds into living areas. Rainwater that touched the ground outside is treated as contaminated.

Mobile and manufactured homes

Water runs under the floor into the belly wrap and particle board subfloor, where it can't be seen. See mobile home water damage.

Water Removal After a Storm or Flood in Lee County

After hurricanes and heavy rain, many Lee County homes have rising water at floor level and roof leaks overhead at the same time. Floodwater and storm surge are Category 3, so removal comes with flood cuts, disinfection and drying.

  • Wait until officials say it is safe to go back in.
  • Keep the power off if water reached outlets or the electrical panel.
  • Never run a generator inside the house, in the garage or near open windows.
  • Photograph everything, inside and out, before you start.
  • Keep children and pets out of rooms that took in floodwater.

Saltwater Storm Surge: Why Water Removal Is Different Near the Gulf

Storm surge in Lee County is salt or brackish water pushed in from the Gulf, up the Caloosahatchee and into the back bays. Homes on Fort Myers Beach, along the river and on Cape Coral canals are the most exposed. Surge does more than wet the house:

  • Mud and silt come with it. Surge leaves a layer of mud that has to be shoveled and washed out before extraction can finish.
  • Salt stays behind. Salt left in drywall, wood and insulation keeps pulling moisture from humid air, so porous materials the surge touched are usually removed rather than dried.
  • Surfaces get washed, not just dried. Studs, block walls and the slab are cleaned and rinsed to take off salt and silt before drying starts.
  • Metal corrodes fast. Outlets, wiring, the electrical panel, AC equipment and appliances that sat in saltwater corrode quickly. Have a qualified electrician check them before the power goes back on.

If surge reached your home, photograph the water line on the walls before anything is cut out. It shows your flood insurer and your building department how high the water rose.

Questions About Emergency Water Removal in Fort Myers

What is emergency water removal?

It is the first stage of water damage restoration: standing water is extracted, soaked materials that can't be saved are removed, moisture is mapped and drying equipment is set so the structure can dry.

How long does water extraction take?

Extraction for one or two rooms usually takes a few hours. Drying the structure afterward usually takes about 3 to 5 days for clean water, longer for slabs, hardwood and contaminated water.

Is water removal the same as water mitigation?

Water removal is the first part of mitigation. Mitigation is the whole job of stopping further damage: extracting water, removing what can't be saved and drying the structure.

Can water removal start before the plumber fixes the leak?

Yes, as long as the water is shut off. Close the valve at the fixture or the main, and extraction can start while you wait for the plumber to repair the pipe.

Does water removal get the water inside the walls?

Not by itself. Extraction pulls the liquid water off floors and out of carpet. Water already inside drywall, behind baseboards and in the slab comes out during structural drying, sometimes with small openings at the bottom of the wall so air can reach it.

Why does my floor feel dry when the meter says it is wet?

Tile and vinyl plank dry on top long before the slab underneath does. A moisture meter reads what is inside the material, which is why drying runs until the readings are normal, not until the surface feels dry.

Can you remove water from a garage or lanai?

Yes. Garage slabs often sit lower than the house, so water from a water heater, washer or storm runoff tends to pool there first. Standing water is extracted, stored boxes are lifted and checked, and the drywall on the wall shared with the house is read with a moisture meter, because water wicks into it from the garage side. On a screened lanai, water is pushed off the slab away from the house so it doesn't run back under the sliding doors.

Will my carpet be saved?

Carpet soaked by clean water and extracted quickly can often be dried in place, but the pad is usually replaced. Carpet touched by sewage or floodwater has to be removed.

Do I need to leave the house during water extraction?

Usually not for a clean-water leak. Extraction is loud for a few hours and the drying equipment runs day and night after that, but most families stay. Leave the affected rooms if the water is sewage or floodwater, or if a ceiling is sagging. See what to do about a leaking ceiling.

Can you get water out from under tile or vinyl plank?

Tile set on a concrete slab usually stays in place: water is pulled from the surface and grout lines, and the slab dries underneath with dehumidifiers over the following days. Floating vinyl plank and laminate trap water beneath them, so they often have to be lifted for the water and the slab under them to dry. Moisture readings decide which boards come up.

Can I run my AC during water removal?

Yes, if the leak was clean water and the AC isn't the source. The AC helps pull humidity out of the air. Keep it off if the water was sewage or floodwater, or if the AC is the thing leaking.

Is storm surge water harder to clean up than pipe water?

Yes. Surge is Category 3 and usually salt or brackish, so porous materials it touched are removed, surfaces are washed to take off salt and silt, and electrical parts are checked before the power returns.

Do you need my gate code or condo manager's number?

It helps. In gated communities and condo buildings, having the gate code and the manager's number ready saves time at the door and lets a shared water line be shut off quickly.
