Key facts about manufactured home water damage

  • Homes built after June 15, 1976 are built to the federal HUD code and called manufactured homes; older ones are usually called mobile homes. Both share the same weak points.
  • Particle board decking swells and loses strength when wet and usually can't be dried back to sound condition.
  • A leak in the belly may never show inside, only as a sagging bottom board or a rising water bill.
  • Lee County has many manufactured-home communities, especially in North Fort Myers, parts of Fort Myers and around Bonita Springs and Estero.

Water Leaking in Your Fort Myers Mobile Home? Do This First

  • Shut off the water. Most manufactured homes have a main valve where the supply line comes up under the home, and many also have a valve at the water heater.
  • Turn off the water heater at its breaker if it's leaking or the water is off.
  • Cut power at the breaker to rooms where water reaches outlets or appliances.
  • Stay off soft spots. Wet particle board can give way under weight.
  • Look underneath from outside with a flashlight: is the bottom board sagging, torn or dripping?
  • Photograph everything, inside and under the home, before cleanup.

Why Water Damage Is Different in a Fort Myers Manufactured Home

A slab house sends water across concrete. A manufactured home sits on a steel frame above the ground, and every layer under your feet can hold water.

Layer, from the top downWhat water does there
Floor covering (carpet, vinyl, plank)Hides the wet decking underneath
Floor decking (particle board, OSB or plywood)Soaks up water, swells and loses strength
Floor joistsStay wet when the decking and insulation around them are wet
Floor insulationSoaks up water like a sponge and holds it against the floor
Ducts and water linesRun through the floor or belly; a leak can flood the ducts
Belly wrap (bottom board)Holds the insulation in place, and holds water from any leak above it

That is why a leak can look small inside while the belly underneath is full of water.

Signs of Hidden Water Damage in a Fort Myers Mobile Home

  • A soft, spongy or bouncy floor near the toilet, tub, sinks or water heater
  • A belly wrap that sags, bulges or drips
  • A musty smell from floor vents when the AC runs
  • Wall panels that are stained, swollen or pulling away at the bottom
  • A water bill that jumps with no visible leak
  • Mold or dark stains along the floor edges
  • Doors that start sticking because the floor has moved

Can a Soft Mobile Home Floor in Fort Myers Be Dried Instead of Replaced?

Sometimes. If clean water was caught within a day or two and the decking is still firm, it can often be dried in place. Particle board that has swelled, crumbled or turned soft has lost its strength and needs to be replaced; walking on it can make it worse. Plywood caught early is the most likely to be saved.

After a Flood: Piers, Tie-Downs and Ducts in Lee County Manufactured Homes

Floodwater or a long leak under the home does more than soak the belly.

  • Piers and tie-downs. Water can wash out the soil under piers and loosen anchors. Have a qualified manufactured-home installer check them before the home is lived in again.
  • Ducts. Ducts in the belly that took floodwater are usually replaced, not cleaned.
  • Electrical. Wiring and junction boxes in the floor system that took floodwater should be checked by a qualified electrician before the power goes back on.
  • Everything in the belly. Floodwater is Category 3, so wet insulation and bottom board come out, and the framing is cleaned and dried.

Insurance for Manufactured Homes in Fort Myers

This is general information, not advice about your policy. Manufactured homes are usually insured under policies written for them, and the coverage rules are similar to homeowners policies:

  • Sudden leaks, such as a burst supply line or a failed water heater, are often covered for the resulting damage.
  • Slow leaks that rotted the floor over months are often excluded as gradual damage or wear and tear.
  • Flood needs a separate flood policy. Under the NFIP, a manufactured home counts as a building when it is affixed to a permanent foundation.
  • Mold coverage is often capped.

Photograph the floor and the belly before anything is removed, keep the plumber's invoice and report the claim promptly. Read how insurance handles water damage in Florida.

How to Protect a Fort Myers Manufactured Home From Water Damage

  • Check the floor around the toilet, tub and water heater every few months for softness.
  • Look under the home after heavy rain and storms for a sagging bottom board.
  • Replace old supply lines and add a water heater pan with a drain.
  • Keep roof seams, vents and the marriage line sealed, especially before hurricane season.
  • Make sure the AC condensate line drains away from the home, not under it.
  • Shut off the water at the main valve when you leave for the season.

Questions About Mobile Home Water Damage in Fort Myers

Why is my mobile home floor soft?

Usually because water has soaked the particle board or OSB decking, most often around a toilet, tub, sink or water heater. Once it softens, it has lost strength and typically needs replacing.

How do I know if there's water in my mobile home's belly?

Look under the home for a sagging, bulging or dripping bottom board, and check for a musty smell from floor vents. A rising water bill with no visible leak is another sign.

Can water in the belly wrap cause mold?

Yes. Wet insulation trapped in the belly stays damp in Florida humidity, which can lead to mold and odors. Opening the belly and drying it quickly prevents that.

Can a water-damaged mobile home floor be repaired?

Particle board that has swelled or gone soft usually has to be cut out and replaced. Plywood caught early can often be dried in place.

Is standing water under my mobile home a problem?

Yes. Water under the home raises humidity in the belly and floor system and invites mold and rot. Find the source, whether a leaking line, an AC drain or poor drainage, and get the belly area dried.

Does insurance cover a rotted mobile home floor?

It depends. A sudden leak is often covered; rot from a slow leak over months is often excluded.

Is my mobile home covered by flood insurance?

Only if you have a flood policy. Under the NFIP, a manufactured home is covered as a building when it is affixed to a permanent foundation, so check how yours is installed and what your policy says.

Who repairs the bottom board and skirting?

The bottom board is opened and patched or replaced as part of the drying and repair. Skirting is taken off for access and put back afterward; check whether your community has rules for it.

How long does it take to fix water damage in a mobile home?

Drying usually takes several days once the belly is opened. Replacing decking, insulation and the bottom board adds time.
