Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Fort Myers?

Often yes, when the water escaped suddenly: a burst supply line, a failed water heater or an AC pan overflowing from an attic air handler. Often no, when it came from a slow leak, rising floodwater without a flood policy, or a sewer backup without the right endorsement. This guide explains how Florida policies usually handle each case, what Lee County homeowners should check on their own policy and what Florida law requires of insurers.

Key facts about water damage insurance in Florida

  • Sudden and accidental is the rule behind most decisions; gradual leaks are a common exclusion.
  • Flood needs a separate policy from the NFIP or a private flood insurer, and Citizens now requires flood coverage on its home policies that include wind.
  • Sewer and drain backup usually needs a water backup endorsement.
  • Condo owners split coverage with the association under Florida Statute 718.111(11).
  • New and reopened claims: generally one year from the date of loss; supplemental claims: generally 18 months.
  • Your insurer generally must acknowledge your claim within 7 days and pay or deny within 60 days.

Sudden vs Gradual: The Rule Behind Most Fort Myers Water Claims

Most Florida water claims turn on one question: did the water escape suddenly, or did it leak over time?

  • Sudden and accidental: a supply line bursts, a water heater fails, an AC pan overflows. These are the losses policies are built to cover.
  • Gradual, continuous or repeated: a slow drip under a sink, a shower pan leaking into the slab for months, a cast iron drain seeping under the floor. Many policies exclude this.

In a humid Fort Myers home, a slow leak usually leaves its own evidence: a musty smell, swollen baseboards or a stain that spread over weeks. Adjusters look at exactly those signs. Report the date you discovered the damage truthfully and don't guess at how long it was leaking. The plumber's report, photos, moisture readings and water bills show what happened.

Flood vs Water Damage for Lee County Homes

Standard Florida homeowners policies don't cover flooding, meaning surface water that rises from outside: storm surge, an overflowing canal, a flooded street or heavy rain pooling against the house.

  • Flood insurance comes from the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private flood insurer.
  • New NFIP policies usually have a 30-day waiting period, so buying one when a storm is already forecast is usually too late.
  • Wind-driven rain through a storm-damaged roof or window is usually a homeowners claim, while rising water is a flood claim. After hurricanes, adjusters often have to separate the two in the same house, so photograph the water line on the walls before cleanup.
  • In FEMA flood zones that start with A or V, lenders usually require flood insurance on a mortgaged home.

Citizens Policies in Lee County: Water Limits and the Flood Requirement

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida's state-backed insurer, covers many Lee County homes. Its policies have rules worth knowing before you hire anyone.

  • Free emergency water removal. Citizens offers free water removal and drying through its own program for eligible non-weather water losses, like a burst pipe or an appliance leak.
  • A $3,000 limit if you use your own company. Citizens' published materials say that if you choose your own mitigation contractor instead, emergency water mitigation is limited to $3,000, less any applicable deductible.
  • A $10,000 limit on repairs outside its program. For non-weather water losses, permanent repairs done outside its Managed Repair Contractor Network can be capped at $10,000, and that cap is reduced by what was paid for outside mitigation.
  • Flood insurance is becoming mandatory. Under Florida law, Citizens home policies that include wind coverage need a separate flood policy. The requirement has been phasing in by dwelling value, and from January 1, 2027 it applies to all of them, in every flood zone. Condo unit-owner policies, renters policies and policies without wind coverage are not included.

If Citizens insures your home, call Citizens or check your policy before any work beyond emergency water removal starts.

Sewer and Drain Backup Coverage for Fort Myers Homes

Water and sewage that back up through a toilet, tub or floor drain are often excluded from standard policies unless you added a water backup or sewer backup endorsement. That endorsement usually has its own limit, often far below the dwelling limit, so check the dollar amount on your declarations page. In older Fort Myers neighborhoods with cast iron drains, it is one of the most useful endorsements to carry. See sewage backup cleanup.

Condo Water Damage Insurance in Fort Myers: Association vs HO-6

Under Florida Statute 718.111(11), the condo association's policy generally covers the building as originally installed. Unit owners generally insure their own floor, wall and ceiling coverings, electrical fixtures, appliances, water heaters, cabinets, countertops and personal property, usually with an HO-6 policy.

  • If the leak started in another owner's unit, that owner's insurer may also be involved.
  • The association's master policy has its own deductible, and associations can pass some losses on to owners through assessments. Loss assessment coverage on an HO-6 policy can help with that.
  • Condo unit-owner policies are not part of the Citizens flood requirement.

See condo water damage.

Water Damage Limits and Endorsements on Fort Myers Policies

Many Florida homeowners policies now carry a limited water damage endorsement, sometimes called an LWD endorsement or a water damage limitation. It is most common on older homes, it is sometimes chosen for a lower premium, and it can be added at renewal without the homeowner noticing.

  • The cap. It limits what the policy pays for certain water losses, such as a plumbing leak or an appliance discharge, to a fixed amount per loss. A $10,000 limit per loss is common, even when the dwelling limit is far higher.
  • Tear-out. Some endorsements say the cap also covers tearing out and replacing walls or floors to reach the pipe; others don't mention tear-out. Florida appeals courts have read the exact wording of each endorsement to decide which applies.
  • Exclusion plus endorsement. It often sits alongside a water damage exclusion, so the endorsement may be the only coverage for that kind of loss.
  • Emergency measures limits. Some policies cap emergency water mitigation, like the Citizens $3,000 limit above.
  • Matching limits. Under Florida Statute 626.9744, when replaced items don't match in quality, color or size, a homeowners insurer must make reasonable repairs or replacement in adjoining areas, unless the policy says otherwise. Some newer policies limit matching, which matters for tile or vinyl plank that runs through the whole house.
  • Mold limits. Often a small fixed amount.

How to check your policy

  • Look at the declarations page and the endorsement list for words like "Limited Water Damage", "Water Damage Limitation", "Water Damage Exclusion" or "Matching".
  • Ask your agent: "Is water damage from a plumbing leak capped on my policy, and does the cap include tear-out?"
  • Read every renewal, because endorsements are sometimes added there.

When coverage is capped, keeping the scope to what the moisture readings support matters even more. Ask for the written scope before any work beyond emergency water removal, and send a copy to your adjuster.

Deductibles and Policy Terms Fort Myers Homeowners Should Know

  • All other perils (AOP) deductible. The usual deductible for leaks and appliance failures.
  • Hurricane deductible. Florida insurers must offer hurricane deductible options of $500, 2%, 5% or 10% of the dwelling limit, with some exceptions for higher-value homes, and the dollar amount must appear on your declarations page.
  • Once per calendar year. On a personal residential policy, the hurricane deductible applies on an annual basis to all hurricane losses in the same calendar year with the same insurer, not once per storm.
  • What it means in dollars. On a home insured for $400,000, a 2% hurricane deductible is $8,000 and a 5% deductible is $20,000.
  • Separate water deductibles. Some Florida policies carry their own deductible or limit for non-weather water losses.
  • Roof settlement and wind exclusions. Some Florida policies pay for roofs on a depreciated schedule, and some exclude wind entirely, which sends a wind-driven roof leak to a separate wind policy. Check both before storm season.

What Your Insurer Must Do for a Fort Myers Claim Under Florida Law

Florida Statute 627.70131 sets deadlines for residential property claims:

StepFlorida deadline
Acknowledge your claim communicationWithin 7 calendar days
Begin investigatingWithin 7 days after receiving your proof-of-loss statement
Physical inspection, if one is neededWithin 30 days after receiving your proof-of-loss statement
Send you a copy of any detailed loss estimateWithin 7 days after the adjuster prepares it
Pay or deny all or part of the claimWithin 60 days after notice, unless factors beyond the insurer's control prevent it
  • The insurer must explain in writing why it paid, denied or partly denied the claim, and explain the difference when it pays less than a detailed estimate.
  • The adjuster who inspects must identify themselves to you in writing.
  • Within 14 days after you first report a claim, your insurer must send you the Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights, a plain-language summary of these rights.

Keep a log of every call, email and adjuster visit, with dates and names. Questions can go to the Florida Department of Financial Services Insurance Consumer Helpline at 1-877-693-5236.

Assignment of Benefits Is Gone: Who Pays Whom in Fort Myers Now

An assignment of benefits (AOB) used to let homeowners sign insurance benefits over to a contractor, who then billed the insurer directly. For residential and commercial property policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, Florida law bars assigning post-loss benefits.

  • Your contract for the work is between you and the company you hire.
  • Your insurer pays under your policy, often with checks made out to you and sometimes your mortgage company too.
  • Mortgage companies often hold large checks and release the money in stages as repairs are done.
  • Be cautious with anyone who asks you to sign over your benefits.

How to Protect Your Fort Myers Water Damage Claim

  • Stop the water and make it safe.
  • Photograph and video everything before cleanup: the source, every wet room, the water line on the walls and damaged belongings.
  • Take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Emergency water removal and structural drying count, and waiting for an adjuster before drying usually makes the damage worse.
  • Keep damaged materials or photos of them, and a list of damaged belongings with their age and what they were worth.
  • Keep moisture readings and drying logs. They show what was wet and when it was dry.
  • Keep the invoice from the plumber, roofer or HVAC technician that says what failed.
  • Report the claim promptly, and write down your claim number and the adjuster's name.
  • Be there for the adjuster's inspection and walk them through the moisture map.
  • Ask for written scopes before work beyond emergency water removal, and send copies to your adjuster.

Will Insurance Pay for a Hotel While Your Fort Myers Home Dries?

Many homeowners policies include additional living expense or loss of use coverage, which can pay for a hotel, meals and other extra living expenses if a covered loss makes your home unlivable. Sewage, a flooded kitchen or the only bathroom out of service are common reasons. Keep every receipt and ask your adjuster before booking a long stay.

Seasonal and Vacant Homes in Lee County

Many policies limit some coverage when a home sits vacant or unoccupied for a set number of consecutive days, and some expect the water to be shut off while you're away. Read that section of your policy before you leave for the season. Home watch reports, leak sensor logs and normal utility records help show when damage happened. See water damage in vacant homes.

Older Fort Myers Homes: Plumbing and Insurability

Many Florida insurers ask for a four-point inspection on older homes, covering the roof, electrical, plumbing and HVAC. Older plumbing, like polybutylene supply lines or cast iron drains, can make coverage harder to get or lead to exclusions. Replacing it can help with insurability and lowers the risk of the next leak. See cast iron pipe water damage and slab leak water damage.

If Your Fort Myers Water Damage Claim Is Denied or Underpaid

  • Read the letter. Find the exact policy language the insurer relied on.
  • Ask for the reports. Request the adjuster's report and any engineer or plumber reports.
  • Send what's missing. Photos, plumber reports, moisture logs, receipts and water bills.
  • Free mediation. The Florida Department of Financial Services runs a mediation program for many residential property claim disputes.
  • Appraisal. Many policies have an appraisal clause for disputes about the amount of the loss.
  • Public adjusters. They negotiate claims for a fee. Florida caps their fees at 10% of payments on claims from a declared emergency in the first year, and 20% on other claims.
  • Before a lawsuit. Florida requires written notice of intent to sue at least 10 business days before filing, and only after the insurer has made a coverage decision. The insurer must respond in writing within 10 business days.
  • Coverage disputes. Talk with a Florida property insurance attorney.

Questions About Water Damage Insurance in Fort Myers

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in Florida?

Usually yes for the water damage the burst causes, such as wet drywall, flooring and cabinets. Repairing the pipe itself may not be covered.

Does insurance cover AC leak water damage in Fort Myers?

Often for sudden overflows that damage ceilings, walls and floors. The AC repair itself usually isn't covered, and leaks that ran for weeks may be excluded.

Is a slab leak covered by homeowners insurance?

The resulting damage to floors, walls and cabinets often is, when the leak was sudden. The pipe repair may not be, though some policies pay to break out the slab to reach it.

Is mold from a water leak covered?

Sometimes, usually up to a mold limit, and usually only when the mold came from a covered water loss.

How long do I have to file a water damage claim in Florida?

Florida law generally requires notice of a new claim within one year of the date of loss, and supplemental claims within 18 months. Your policy may also require prompt notice.

Can a restoration company bill my insurance directly in Florida?

Not through an assignment of benefits on policies issued on or after January 1, 2023. Your contract is with the company you hire, and your insurer pays under your policy.

Do I have to use my insurer's water removal program?

Usually not, but some policies limit what they pay if you don't. Citizens, for example, limits emergency water mitigation to $3,000 for eligible non-weather losses when you use your own company instead of its free program. Check your policy before work starts.

Does my Citizens policy require flood insurance?

If your Citizens home policy includes wind coverage, yes, under a phased Florida requirement that reaches all of those policies on January 1, 2027. Condo unit-owner and renters policies are not included.

What is the $10,000 water damage limit on Florida policies?

It usually comes from a limited water damage endorsement that caps what the policy pays for certain water losses, often plumbing leaks, per loss. Whether the cap also covers tearing out walls or floors depends on the endorsement's wording.

Will my premium go up if I file a water damage claim?

It can. Insurers may look at claims history at renewal. Ask your agent before filing a small claim close to your deductible.

My insurer says the leak was gradual. What now?

Ask for the denial in writing with the policy language it relies on. Gather what shows when the leak started, such as the plumber's report, photos, moisture readings and water bills. You can request free mediation through the Florida Department of Financial Services and talk to a public adjuster or attorney.

Where can I get free help with a Florida insurance claim?

Call the Florida Department of Financial Services Insurance Consumer Helpline at 1-877-693-5236. It answers questions about claims and runs the state's mediation program for many residential disputes.
