Water Damage Restoration in Fort Myers, Step by Step

  1. 1

    You call

    When you call 239-356-3708, you'll be asked a few quick questions:

    • Your address and ZIP code
    • What happened and when you found it
    • Where the water came from: a supply line, appliance, AC, toilet, drain, roof or flooding
    • Which rooms are wet and roughly how big the wet area is
    • Whether the water and power are off
    • Whether you own or rent, and whether it's a condo or a gated community
    • Your insurance company, if you plan to file a claim

    If the water is still running, you'll be walked through shutting it off. Timing is confirmed with you before anyone heads out.

  2. 2

    While you wait

    • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main. On a private well, switch off the pump too.
    • Cut power at the breaker to wet rooms.
    • Photograph and video everything before you move or throw anything away.
    • Move belongings off wet floors.
    • Keep the AC running to help pull moisture out of the air, unless it's the source or the water is sewage or floodwater.

    The full list: first-hour water damage checklist.

  3. 3

    The inspection

    • The source is confirmed stopped, or you're told who needs to fix it: a plumber, roofer or HVAC technician.
    • The water category is identified: clean (Category 1), gray (Category 2) or contaminated, such as sewage or floodwater (Category 3). This decides what can be dried and what has to be removed.
    • Moisture is measured in floors, walls, cabinets and ceilings with moisture meters and a thermal camera.
    • The wet area is mapped; it's usually bigger than what you can see.
    • Findings are explained in plain language, and you can see the readings.
  4. 4

    The written scope

    Before work beyond emergency water removal, you get the scope in writing. It should explain:

    • What will be removed and why
    • What equipment will be used and for about how long
    • The price, based on square footage, water category, materials and drying days
    • What isn't included, such as plumbing repairs or rebuilding

    If more water is found during drying, such as inside a wall, the scope is updated in writing with photos and readings. For general background on what moves the price, see the water damage pricing guide.

  5. 5

    Water removal

    Standing water is pulled out with truck-mounted or portable extractors. Extraction removes far more water than drying ever could, which shortens drying and limits damage. See emergency water removal.

  6. 6

    Removing what can't be saved

    • Carpet pad soaked with water
    • Baseboards that have swelled
    • The lower part of drywall when it's soaked, contaminated or hiding water
    • Wet insulation inside walls or ceilings
    • Particle board cabinet bases and shelves that have swelled
    • Vinyl plank or laminate trapping water against the slab

    Everything removed is photographed and listed for your records and your insurer.

  7. 7

    Drying

    Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up to dry the structure. Moisture readings are taken and logged, and equipment is moved as areas dry. Most clean-water jobs take about 3 to 5 days; floodwater, sewage, concrete slabs and long-running leaks take longer. See structural drying.

    During drying:

    • Equipment runs around the clock and makes noise.
    • Keep doors and windows closed unless told otherwise.
    • Keep the AC running.
    • Don't turn equipment off without asking.
  8. 8

    Final readings and paperwork

    When readings show materials are back to normal, drying is done. You receive:

    • Photos from before, during and after
    • The moisture map and drying logs
    • A list of materials removed
    • A final invoice that matches the written scope, or explains any changes

    These are the records an insurance adjuster usually wants to see.

  9. 9

    Repairs

    After drying, drywall, baseboards, flooring and cabinets are rebuilt. Rebuilding is your decision, and you choose who does it.

Why Fort Myers Humidity Changes the Drying Plan

Outdoor air in Southwest Florida is humid for much of the year, so opening windows usually slows drying instead of helping it. That's why doors and windows stay closed, the AC keeps running and dehumidifiers do the heavy lifting. It's also why a leak left for a few days here can lead to mold faster than in drier climates.

What Changes for Fort Myers Condos, Rentals and Businesses

  • Condos: building management is told right away, and units above or below may need readings too. See condo water damage.
  • Rentals: the landlord or property manager is part of the plan from the first call.
  • Gated communities: gate access is arranged before anyone heads out.
  • Businesses: drying can often be sectioned around your hours. See commercial water damage.

What You Won't Be Asked to Do in Your Fort Myers Home

  • Sign over your insurance benefits. For Florida residential policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, post-loss assignment of benefits isn't allowed.
  • Pay for work you didn't agree to in writing.
  • Decide on the spot about rebuilding.

Mitigation, Restoration, Remediation: Words You'll Hear From Fort Myers Adjusters

You'll hear these words from adjusters and contractors, and they often appear as separate parts of an estimate or a claim.

  • Mitigation: the emergency work that stops the damage getting worse. Water removal, taking out what can't be saved, and drying.
  • Restoration: putting the home back. New drywall, baseboards, flooring, cabinets and paint once the structure is dry.
  • Remediation: removing a contaminant, usually mold or sewage, inside containment.
  • Moisture mapping: measuring and recording where the water went, wall by wall and room by room.
  • Dry standard: the reading that unaffected materials of the same kind show in your home. Drying is finished when the wet materials reach it.

Insurers often handle mitigation and repairs as separate parts of the claim, so ask for separate paperwork for each.

What the Water Category Means for Your Fort Myers Job

These categories come from the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

CategoryExamplesWhat usually happens
Category 1: clean waterBurst supply line, water heater, AC condensate caught earlyMost materials can be dried in place if drying starts quickly
Category 2: gray waterWasher or dishwasher drain water, toilet overflow without wasteCarpet pad and porous materials often removed; surfaces cleaned and treated
Category 3: contaminated waterSewage, floodwater, storm surgePorous materials removed; surfaces disinfected; protective gear used

A clean leak left for days can become Category 2, which is why starting the same day matters.

What the Drying Equipment Does in a Fort Myers Home

  • Air movers blow air across wet surfaces so moisture evaporates faster.
  • Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air so it doesn't soak back into walls and floors.
  • Specialty equipment can push dry air into wall cavities, under cabinets and across concrete slabs.
  • Moisture meters and thermal cameras track progress, so drying ends when readings say it's dry, not when it looks dry.

Mold After Water Damage in Fort Myers Homes

If mold has already grown, it should be removed by a qualified Florida mold remediation company. Fast drying is the most reliable way to prevent mold in the first place.

Questions About the Water Damage Restoration Process in Fort Myers

Who will come to my home?

A water damage technician who works in your area. Details and timing are confirmed with you before anyone arrives.

Do I get the price before work starts?

Yes. You get a written scope before work beyond emergency water removal begins.

Do I have to use the same company for repairs?

No. Rebuilding is your decision, and you choose who does it.

Will you deal with my insurance company?

You file and manage your claim. The photos, moisture readings and drying logs from the job are there to support it.

Do I need to be home during drying?

Someone needs to give access for setup and readings. You don't have to be there all day while the equipment runs.

Why does the drying equipment have to run overnight?

Wet materials keep releasing moisture around the clock, and turning equipment off lets the air and the walls take it back up, especially in Fort Myers humidity. Steady drying is what gets the job done in days instead of weeks.

What changes if the water is sewage or floodwater?

More comes out and more cleaning happens. Porous materials the water touched are removed, surfaces are disinfected, protective gear is used, and affected rooms may be closed off until the work is done. See sewage backup cleanup.

What if more damage is found during drying?

The scope is updated in writing and explained, with photos and readings showing what was found.
